## 0:50 The skills you're undervaluing

Before the list, one thing SLPs get wrong about themselves.

You have explained phonological awareness to a parent who had never heard the phrase. That is UX writing.

You have sold a treatment plan to a family that did not want to hear it. That is medical device sales.

You have run an IEP meeting with a parent who came in angry. Job postings call that "managing difficult stakeholders," and they pay for it.

You have never been short on skills. You've been using the wrong words for them. Keep that in mind, because most of what follows runs on skills you already have.

## 1:30 Tier one: your license works today

These roles use the license you already hold. One to three months of job searching, no new credential.

Clinical liaison. The most-posted non-clinical title for rehab professionals: eighty-three separate postings in the first quarter of 2025 alone. You visit hospitals and physician offices, explain your facility's programs, and screen patients for admission. Eighty-four to a hundred and thirty-five thousand, often with bonuses. The catch is travel, and some roles carry a census target.

Utilization review. You read therapy documentation and decide whether it meets coverage criteria. You already know skilled intervention from maintenance, which is the whole job. Usually fully remote, no cancellations, eighty to eighty-eight thousand. The catch: some roles are about cost containment, and that will rub against your clinical instincts.

Case manager. Number one title in the third quarter of 2025. You coordinate care between providers, patients and families, which is discharge planning at scale. Fifty-four to eighty-two thousand without a nursing license, higher at insurers and in government. A CCM certification helps, and plenty of employers hire SLPs without it.

Clinical educator at an AAC or voice company. Tobii Dynavox, PRC-Saltillo, Lingraphica, Passy-Muir. They need clinicians who teach clinicians to use the product. Seventy-five to a hundred and sixteen thousand. Every device you have ever trained a colleague on is your qualification. Check the travel percentage before you fall for the posting.

Rehab management. Clinical manager, program manager, then director of rehab. You already run the parts of a department nobody bills for: the schedule, the coverage, the audit, the new grad who is drowning. Management pays for that. Eighty-two to a hundred and thirty-nine thousand across the ladder. The door is usually internal, so ask your director who the last two manager openings went to.

Clinical research coordinator. The fastest way onto a résumé that no longer reads as purely clinical, and the one path here that pays less than you make now: forty-eight to seventy-two thousand. Two documented SLPs used it as a springboard, one into pharma patient safety with a raise inside two years. Take it as a first step. Never as a destination.

## 3:30 Tier two: six to twelve months of building

These need a portfolio, a course, or both.

Content and copywriting. You've spent years translating jargon for people who never asked for a glossary. Build three writing samples, even in a Google Doc, and offer them to SLP companies and education startups first. Experienced health writers earn eighty to a hundred and forty-one thousand, usually remote.

Customer success. Onboarding, training, troubleshooting, and keeping a relationship warm with someone who is stressed. Teletherapy platforms, EMR companies and AAC makers hire former clinicians for exactly this. Seventy-five to a hundred and twenty thousand, base plus bonus.

Clinical informatics. If you have strong opinions about how bad your EMR is, this is where those opinions become a job. You configure the system, train the staff, and translate between IT and clinicians. Ninety-eight to a hundred and fifty-four thousand. One warning: nobody can sell you an Epic certification. It comes only through an employer who sponsors you, so the route in is a sponsor-track junior analyst role or go-live support. The credential you can earn on your own is CAHIMS.

UX research and writing. Your assessment training is a user interview with a different name. Bethany Riebock left medical SLP for UX research and put it in five words: patient-first became user-first. Sixty-seven to a hundred and fifty-four thousand in health tech. The honest part: this is the most crowded path on the list, about thirty-five percent more graduates over five years against flat openings. Two to four deep case studies beat any certificate, and health tech is the realistic door, not big tech.

Conversation design. You teach turn-taking, repair strategies and conversational flow to humans. Chatbots and voice assistants need the same lessons. Sixty-eight to a hundred and two thousand, more at the big companies. Your pragmatics background is a genuine edge, and almost nobody else applying has it.

Instructional design. Build the training instead of delivering it. Take one in-service you already gave and rebuild it as a single module. That is sample one, and hiring managers want two or three of those far more than a three-thousand-dollar certificate. Seventy to a hundred thousand.

Medical and ed-tech sales. Sixty-seven to a hundred and seventeen thousand base, up to about one twenty-five with commission. Same persuasion you use on a skeptical family, with a quota attached.

Voice and communication coaching. This one is a business, so there is no salary band. Market rates run two to four hundred an hour and the whole problem is filling the hours. Start it on the side before you count on it.

## 6:00 Tier three: a year or more

Real upskilling, and the biggest jumps.

Software engineering. A former SLP in Austin taught herself to code, did an internship, and now earns about ninety-three thousand at a health-tech startup, more than she ever made clinically. Six to eighteen months. Try the free resources first to find out whether you like it.

Data analyst. Healthcare analytics, SQL and statistics. Seventy to a hundred and five thousand.

Project management. A certificate plus the organizational skills you already have. Eighty-five to a hundred thousand and up.

Forensic SLP. Expert witness work, high hourly rates once you're established, a niche door.

Academia. Clinical instructor and clinic supervisor roles are the realistic entry with a master's. Tenure track usually wants a doctorate. Seventy-eight to a hundred and six thousand full-time equivalent, and adjunct pay never adds up to a salary.

## 6:50 Telehealth

Telehealth is not on the list, on purpose. It is still therapy, and it pays like therapy. It works as a bridge: a part-time contract cuts the commute and the documentation enough to free the hours a transition needs, and the platforms themselves hire former clinicians into implementation and customer success.

## 7:10 Three things people worry about

Do you need to go back to school? For every path in the first two tiers, no. A portfolio, a course, or your license.

Will you take a pay cut? Look at the ranges again. One path on this list pays less than clinical work, and it is labelled. The rest match or beat it, usually with better hours.

What do you say in the interview? Skip the burnout speech. Say what you want to do next, and for every skill in the posting have one SLP example ready. Difficult stakeholders is an IEP meeting. Prioritizing under pressure is a full caseload with notes due Friday.

## 7:50 The market

In 2025, more than a thousand non-clinical jobs were posted every quarter for PT, OT and SLP professionals, and nearly a quarter of them were fully remote. Those postings close fast: historically, about forty-two percent within two weeks. When you see one, apply that week.
