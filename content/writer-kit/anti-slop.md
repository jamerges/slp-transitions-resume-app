# Anti-Slop Editing Rules for SLP Transitions

Use these as diagnostics, not automatic prohibitions.

## Flag aggressively

- generic throat-clearing
- faux profundity
- importance puffery
- unsupported certainty
- empty abstractions
- repeated rhetorical questions
- repetitive dramatic fragments
- synonym cycling
- generic future-of-work language
- corporate verbs such as leverage, empower, facilitate, optimize, streamline, utilize
- conclusions that merely restate the introduction
- transitions that sound polished but add no information

## Be suspicious of templates

Examples:

- "It's not just X. It's Y."
- "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "Here's what nobody tells you."
- "The real question is..."
- "In a world where..."
- "Now more than ever..."
- "Unlock your potential."

These may occasionally be usable, but they carry a high burden of proof.

## Do not erase James

James naturally uses:

- rhetorical questions
- fragments
- contrasts
- self-deprecation
- analogies
- conversational interruptions

Keep them when they are specific, funny, revealing, or useful for rhythm.

## Portability test

Ask:

> Could this sentence or paragraph survive unchanged in an article for teachers, nurses, accountants, or software engineers?

If yes, make it more specific or cut it.

## Final principle

Specificity beats polish.

The objective is not "human-sounding" writing. It is writing that plausibly could have come only from James and SLP Transitions.
