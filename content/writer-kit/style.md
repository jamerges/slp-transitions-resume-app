# SLP Transitions Editorial Standard

## Mission

Help SLPs make better career decisions through:

1. honest lived experience
2. useful mental models
3. credible evidence
4. real transition stories
5. concrete experiments

Do not assume leaving clinical work is the right answer.

## A strong article does three things

### Teaches me something
Give the reader a concept, mechanism, story, dataset, or perspective they did not have.

### Makes me feel understood
Describe the actual experience of being an SLP, not generic career dissatisfaction.

### Changes what I can do next
Offer a question, experiment, resource, conversation, or small action.

## Start with tension

Do not automatically begin by defining the topic.

Start with a scene, contradiction, observation, difficult question, personal experience, or surprising finding.

## Write from specifics outward

Prefer:

> A school SLP with 70 students and six IEP meetings this week...

over:

> Heavy workloads can contribute to burnout.

## Use SLP-native detail

When relevant, use real details such as:

- caseloads
- productivity requirements
- IEPs
- progress notes
- evaluations
- parents
- supervisors
- CF year
- school versus medical settings
- therapy planning
- ASHA
- licensure
- continuing education
- graduate-school debt
- emotional attachment to clients

Do not sprinkle SLP terminology into generic career advice.

## Research standard

Prioritize:

1. primary research
2. systematic reviews and respected academic sources
3. government or professional data
4. strong secondary research organizations
5. expert commentary

Verify attractive statistics.

Avoid pop-neuroscience certainty.

## Interview stories

Preserve the subject's actual personality.

Do not rewrite interviewees until they sound like James.

One person's career transition proves a path exists, not that everyone should follow it.

## Practical advice

Favor experiments over declarations.

Useful:

- interview someone doing the job
- shadow someone
- take on a small project
- build one portfolio piece
- calculate runway
- compare job descriptions
- identify one skill gap
- test an adjacent role

Weak:

- believe in yourself
- embrace change
- follow your passion

## SEO

Write for the human first, then make it discoverable.

Answer search intent, but do not let competitor headings dictate the intellectual structure.

The goal is not merely to rank. It is to create the best answer for the specific SLP question.

## Endings

Prefer:

- returning to the opening with new understanding
- one concrete experiment
- the most interesting consequence of the argument

Avoid generic inspirational summaries and manufactured mic drops.
