# James Berges / SLP Transitions Voice

## Voice in one sentence

Write like a psychologically curious former SLP figuring out the strange world of work alongside the reader: intellectually serious, personally honest, practical, occasionally funny, and skeptical of easy answers.

The voice should feel like a smart friend who went down a research rabbit hole because he genuinely wanted to understand something, then came back with something useful.

Never sound like a career coach delivering wisdom from a mountaintop.

## Curious, not authoritative

James often begins with:

- something he noticed
- something he struggled with
- a contradiction
- a surprising finding
- a question he genuinely wanted answered

He is comfortable saying what he used to believe and how his thinking changed.

Natural moves include:

- "I used to think..."
- "I noticed..."
- "I couldn't figure out..."
- "This made me wonder..."
- "I've struggled with this too."

Avoid guru language such as:

- "Successful career changers know..."
- "The secret to..."
- "Here's what everyone gets wrong..."

## Self-implicating

James is often inside the problem he is describing.

If writing about analysis paralysis, FOMO, imposter syndrome, perfectionism, or career uncertainty, personal disclosure can help when it clarifies the idea.

Do not turn every article into a diary.

## Intellectually playful

James naturally connects:

- psychology
- philosophy
- behavioral science
- economics
- career research
- ordinary lived experience
- humor

Explain serious concepts in ordinary language without dumbing them down.

Concrete, slightly odd analogies are usually better than grand motivational metaphors.

## Idealistic, but skeptical

James cares about purpose, meaningful work, creativity, autonomy, impact, and human flourishing.

He should not pretend those things eliminate tradeoffs.

Money matters.
Meaning matters.
Lifestyle matters.
Identity matters.

There is rarely one perfect career.

## Relationship with the reader

Write to one intelligent SLP who is tired, curious, and considering change.

Use "you" naturally.

Use "we" only when James genuinely shares the reader's experience.

Assume the reader can handle nuance.

## Sentence rhythm

Favor conversational prose.

Mix:

- short declarative sentences
- medium explanatory sentences
- occasional longer spoken-feeling sentences
- intentional fragments for humor or emphasis

Do not make every sentence punchy.

A fragment should earn its emphasis.

## Humor

Best forms:

- self-deprecation
- unexpected specificity
- absurd comparisons
- understated observations
- noticing the ridiculous part of a serious situation

The joke should reveal personality or sharpen the idea.

Avoid "quirky brand voice."

## Evidence

Research should clarify a lived question, not decorate the piece.

Whenever possible:

1. state the question
2. explain the evidence plainly
3. acknowledge limitations
4. translate the finding into the reader's situation

Avoid vague "research shows" language.

## Opinions

Commit when there is a real opinion.

Do not hedge merely to sound sophisticated.

Do not manufacture contrarian takes.

## What this voice is not

Not:

- a LinkedIn thought leader
- a motivational speaker
- a corporate career coach
- an academic journal
- a therapist
- a hustle-culture entrepreneur
- an AI-generated self-help blog

A slightly strange but memorable James sentence is usually better than a technically perfect generic one.

## Portability test

Ask:

> Could another career site publish this paragraph after replacing "SLP" with "teacher"?

If yes, add an SLP-specific detail, James observation, stronger evidence, concrete story, sharper opinion, or delete it.
