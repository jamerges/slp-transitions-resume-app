# Practitioner Pivot, "Non-Clinical Getting Started Bundle": what to take

Read 2026-09-21 from the files James bought: a 51-minute recorded webinar
(transcribed with faster-whisper), the 16-slide deck it runs over, a 39-page
networking guide, and a 3-page résumé translation checklist. Hannah Pugh,
OTR/L, founder of Practitioner Pivot, now a client success manager in
health-tech. Her audience is every clinician (PT, OT, SLP, RN); ours is SLPs.
All notes are in my words. Her files are licensed for personal use and
nothing is reused verbatim.

## What the product is

A folder of files. That is the delivery mechanism, and it is worth noticing
before anything else. Buyers get four things they own: a video to watch
once, a deck to keep, a guide to print, a checklist to hold next to the
résumé. Nothing lives behind a login. Compared with ours, which is a website
with tools that save, hers feels like a purchase and ours feels like access.
Both have costs; hers cannot remember anything about the buyer, ours cannot
be held in the hand. The fix for us is not to abandon the tools but to make
sure every paid tier hands over files as well (the workbook does this; the
rest of the kit below adds two more).

**The spine is one named framework.** PIVOT: Purpose, Inventory, View,
Outreach, Target and Tailor. Every slide, every guide page and the whole
sales argument hang off it, and the argument is one sentence: most people
start at T, the résumé, which is why fifty applications produced two
interviews and no offer. It is a diagnosis the buyer recognises, and the
product is the cure. Ours has the same shape (five stages, eight stops,
Ground before Explore before Connect before Translate) and the same
diagnosis in lesson 6.6, and we have never once put it on the sales page as
the reason to buy.

**The webinar is cheap and does the trust work.** Talking head over slides,
recorded at night in one take with a cough break left in, "tonight's
webinar" acknowledged as a recording. Nobody minds. What it buys is a face
and a voice for fifty minutes, which is the one thing a text course cannot
give a stranger. We have no footage anywhere. This is the single largest
gap between the two products and it is not a content gap.

**The FAQ is the market research.** She polled Instagram and got four
questions: how do I search for jobs and companies, how do I get confident
enough to believe I could do the job, what will I earn, where should I
network. Those are the questions, and she answers three of them without a
number. We can answer all four with data we already hold (the twenty paths
with sourced ranges, the 188 companies, the open roles page, the stage
map). That is the differentiator and it should be said out loud.

## Copy and voice

What works, and why:

- **Permission language, plainly.** "You have permission to outgrow the
  environments that no longer serve you." "A pivot is not a sign your
  clinical career failed; it is a sign your skills have grown beyond your
  setting." Warm, first person, said once, not belaboured. Ours says the
  same in the five-stages article and lesson 1.1; hers is shorter.
- **One image for the whole idea.** Your clinical training lives inside a
  small ecosystem that sits inside a much larger economy full of companies
  that need it. Then the question swap: stop asking "am I qualified?" and
  ask "where do my skills create value?"
- **The marketing-flyer analogy.** Starting at the résumé without knowing
  where you are going is printing a flyer for a company you have not named.
- **The colleague test.** If a bullet reads like a task list any colleague
  could have written, that is your sign. (Added to 4.2 on 2026-09-21.)
- **The number litmus.** Put a number on the résumé only if you could
  explain in the interview how you got it. We already have the ten-minute
  version in 4.2.
- **Search order.** Industry, then company, then person, then posting,
  never "non-clinical SLP" into a job board. (Added to 3.1.)
- **Confidence is built, not discovered.** It comes after action, and the
  action is conversations, exposure and small wins.
- **Total compensation and trajectory** beat the base number. We have it in
  6.5.
- **Start with the vendors who demo in your clinic** and the products you
  already use. (Added to 3.1 as "what is already on your cart".)
- **"What do you do for work?"** as a free informational interview with
  seven follow-ups. (Added to 3.1.)
- **Make the ask cheap:** one direct question, a booking link or two time
  windows, a one-question fallback, and the four-check critique of real
  messages she received. (Added to 3.2 with a send self-check tool.)
- **Offer something back** at the end of every call. (Added to the 3.2
  thank-you.)

What does not, and what we do instead:

- **The translations are the slop we warn about.** "Orchestrated daily
  end-to-end client workflows for premium accounts, maintaining 100% data
  compliance" for "managed a caseload." "Strategic interventions to drive
  goal adoption." Our 4.2 and 4.4 exist to stop exactly this, and 4.2 now
  shows the swap beside the translation so a buyer sees the difference.
- **Unsourced rules stated as facts.** 500+ connections makes you look
  legitimate; LinkedIn is a 100 percent requirement; her salary went up
  every year. None of it is wrong, none of it is evidence, and we do not
  repeat any of it.
- **Stock and generated imagery.** Purple Canva deck, laptops and coffee
  cups, and at least two faces that read as generated. Our rule stays: real
  photos or none.
- **Generic LinkedIn padding.** Six pages on profile photos and cover
  images. A reader who bought a networking guide did not need to be told to
  use good lighting.
- **The salary question dodged.** "It depends." We have ranges with sources
  for twenty paths, and the quiz result puts the right one in front of the
  reader in two minutes. Never dodge it.
- **The "transitioning" framing rule contradicts ours in one place.** She
  says drop clinical credentials from the headline unless the role is
  therapy-adjacent. Our 3.4 keeps CCC-SLP at the end because at speech-tech
  and health-tech companies the licence is the qualification. Keep ours;
  the audiences differ.

## Why Module 1 has not sold, read against this bundle

Nobody has bought Module 1 at $19 or, so far, at $9. The bundle shows why
more clearly than our own numbers do.

1. **It sells reflection; the market buys orientation.** Every competitor
   product at this rung is called some version of Getting Started (hers,
   NCPT's Non-Clinical 101, Teacher Career Coach's first module). The buyer
   at stage three wants to know what exists, what it pays, and what to do
   first. "Your reasons in writing" is true and valuable and it is the part
   nobody wakes up wanting to pay for.
2. **We gave the concrete outputs away.** The money page and the verdict,
   the two most tangible things in Modules 0 and 1, are free. What is left
   behind the $9 is the introspective half. Her free tier is a lead magnet;
   her paid tier is the whole kit.
3. **The name says "not yet."** "Before You Start Looking" tells a stage-4
   reader the product is not for them, and tells a stage-3 reader to wait.
4. **No face, no story, no proof.** Her webinar is fifty minutes of a
   person. Ours is a page.
5. **Three products at three prices was already hard to read;** three at $9
   is a list, not a ladder.

## The repackaging, for James to decide

Turn Module 1 into the kit the market is asking for, without abandoning
what is good in it.

- **Name it for the stage, not the mood.** "Getting Started, for SLPs" or
  "The Starting Line Kit." Keep "Before You Start Looking" as a line inside
  it if you like the phrase.
- **Make the paid tier the whole first month, not one module.** Module 1
  (the reasons and the workbook) plus the first three networking lessons
  (3.1 to 3.3, with the people list) plus the résumé translation pass (4.2
  and the 4.7 checklist) plus the stage map. That is her PIVOT in our
  clothes: purpose, inventory, view, outreach, target. Modules 2, 5, 6 and
  7 stay for the full program. The code change is a per-lesson allowance
  in `canOpen` rather than a per-module one; an afternoon.
- **Hand over files.** The workbook PDF already exists. Add a two-page
  "translation pass" PDF built from 4.2's pairs and 4.7's checklist, and a
  one-page "who to talk to first" sheet from 3.1. Both render from the same
  scripts as the workbook.
- **Record the webinar.** Twenty-five minutes, talking head over our own
  slides, one take, evening is fine. Script drafted at
  `content/course/getting-started-webinar-script.md`, built from our data
  and James's actual biography only. This is the piece that changes trust,
  and it is the one only James can make.
- **Rewrite the sales page around the diagnosis.** Open with "most SLPs
  start at the résumé," show the eight stops as one graphic, answer the four
  FAQ questions with our numbers, then the kit, then the price. Proof stays
  limited to what we have clearance for.
- **Free tier stays free** (Module 0, the verdict, the email link), because
  it is working as a door. What changes is what the door opens onto.
