Source: "SLP alternatives and job transitions" (private, 26,647 members,
~126 posts/month, created 2021). Searched from James's account for
completed-transition language. Engagement counts are that group's.

**Ethics/rules note before any outreach.** Group rule 4 bans self-promotion,
rule 1 declines posts asking for DMs, rule 6 says what's shared in the group
stays in the group. So: no recruiting post in the group, no quoting these
posts on the site, no screenshots. Contact people **individually by DM**, and
treat everything below as a lead, not as publishable material — the interview
itself is what makes a story usable, on their terms.

A structural note: most posts here are pre-transition (asking). Named people
who have actually landed somewhere are rare, and their posts are the
highest-engagement content in the group by a wide margin. That scarcity is
the whole opportunity — this audience is starving for completed stories, and
completed stories are exactly what slptransitions.com publishes.

## Recurring themes worth writing about

Ranked by evidence of demand in this group.

1. **"What degree do you WISH you'd gotten instead of an SLP masters?" — 568
   comments.** The largest thread encountered by an order of magnitude. This is
   a whole article, and arguably a whole tool: the honest version isn't a list
   of degrees, it's "here's which of those actually require the degree and
   which don't." Ties directly to our factual guardrails.
2. **Private equity in pediatric clinics.** A post two days old about Ivy Rehab
   (productivity conversations, AI-usage mandates, chasing families to fill
   visits, 37 comments) with the top reply "It's private equity. Profits for
   shareholders." Nobody in the niche is writing the plain explainer: what
   happens to a clinic after acquisition, and what the tells are. **Zero
   coverage on our site and it's live right now.**
3. **Frozen, not uninformed.** The dominant emotional state is paralysis, not
   ignorance — "I'm just stuck. I'm frozen." People know options exist. This
   argues the $9 report's real job is *sequencing* ("what do I do first"),
   which is how it should be described in copy.
4. **Resume/ATS panic.** A shared .docx resume template drew 274 reactions and
   110 comments. This is the product, validated. It also shows the format
   people trust: a real resume that got real interviews, not advice.
5. **The pay-cut question, and the honest answer.** Universally feared, rarely
   answered with numbers. Kristen Lynch's arc (dip, then past your old salary
   within ~3 years) is the answer, and we have the research-facts bands to
   generalize it.
6. **The research/university on-ramp.** Study coordinator and research
   coordinator roles recur across independent stories (Kristen, Dara) as the
   *first* step out — universities accept varied backgrounds, benefits are
   good, pay is mediocre, and it becomes a springboard. **This is a missing
   ninth path**, and it's the lowest-barrier one found.
7. **Diagnosis during the exit.** Two separate top posts mention discovering
   ADHD or another condition mid-transition and reframing "why is this job
   destroying me" as information rather than failure. Handle carefully, but
   it's a real and unwritten piece.
8. **The CF-year crisis.** Multiple posters realized within months of
   graduating. Content for them looks nothing like content for a 20-year
   veteran — different money, different sunk cost, different shame.
9. **"I don't hate the profession — I hate the 90%."** People keep
   distinguishing the clinical work they love from the documentation,
   productivity, and politics they're fleeing. Our push→pull reframing is
   exactly this, but the post that says it in their words doesn't exist yet.
10. **Acute-care-specific exits** (rotations, department politics, productivity
    metrics) — asked repeatedly, answered thinly.

**One honest correction to our assumptions:** searching this group for
PSLF/student-loan content did **not** surface a loan-specific discussion.
Loans appear as a *trapping* factor inside burnout stories ("hefty school
loans"), not as a standalone question. CLAUDE.md lists PSLF as the biggest
unwritten gap based on reader mail — that may still be true of your list, but
it isn't visible as a discrete theme in this group. Worth a second check
before investing in a pillar post.
