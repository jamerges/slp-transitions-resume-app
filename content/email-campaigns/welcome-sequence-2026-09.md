## E4 · day 7 · "Managed a caseload of 62" vs. "managed a portfolio of 62 accounts"
**Subject:** Same job. Different words. Only one gets interviews.
**Preview:** You don't have a skills problem. You have a translation problem.

Hi {$name|default(there)},

Here's a bullet from a real school SLP's resume:

"Manage caseload of 62 students with IEPs across 3 school buildings"

A hiring manager at an ed-tech company reads that in about 7 seconds (that's the real average) and thinks: clinical person, not for us.

Now the same experience, translated:

"Managed a portfolio of 62 concurrent client accounts across 3 sites, each with individual goals, timelines, and quarterly progress reviews"

Nothing invented. Same Tuesday afternoon: the IEPs, the data collection, the angry-teacher diplomacy. But now it's in words the hiring manager can map to their posting.

This is the whole thing, and almost nobody tells SLPs: you don't have a skills problem. You have a translation problem.

A few more, from SLPs who got hired:
- IEP meetings → cross-functional stakeholder alignment
- Chart audits → quality assurance
- Convincing a skeptical parent to buy into a plan → objection handling (sales and customer-success managers love this one)

The full list, with the exact words:
