Hi {$name|default(there)},

When Osmind raised its Series B, I sent one of their recruiters a note to say congratulations. That was the whole message. No résumé, no "keep me in mind." It started the conversation that became my job.

There's research on why a note like that works: people appreciate being contacted much more than the person reaching out predicts, especially when the message comes out of the blue.

Most SLPs who leave clinical work get there through a person, not a job board. One SLP sent 113 tailored applications over eleven months for a single offer. The moves that go faster almost always have someone on the inside.

If you've got a conversation going with someone who works where you'd like to, here are two questions I'd save for the last two minutes:

**"Is there anyone else you'd talk to if you were me?"**

**"If a [role] opens on your team, would you be open to me sending you the posting?"**

The first hands you the next name. The second is a referral ask in its least awkward form.
