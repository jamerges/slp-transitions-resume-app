Hi {$name|default(there)},

If the hardest part of leaving is a face, the kid on your caseload who finally said the sound, or the patient who asks for you by name, this one's for you.

One SLP on Reddit wrote that leaving mid-year meant "I will be abandoning my students." Some version of that sentence shows up in about eleven of the threads we read.

So I looked up what ASHA actually says. Its ethics guidance defines abandonment as stopping services without arranging other care. It also says leaving for a career move isn't unethical in itself, and that you don't have to stay until they hire your replacement. What counts is the handoff.

I wrote up what that means in practice, including an eight-step checklist for leaving a caseload well and a two-sentence answer for the colleague who says you're making the shortage worse:
