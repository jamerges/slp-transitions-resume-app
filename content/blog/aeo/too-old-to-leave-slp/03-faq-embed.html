Frequently asked questions

Is 40 too old to leave speech therapy?+
No. Americans born between 1957 and 1964 held an average of nearly three jobs between ages 35 and 44, and the moves we've documented from SLP into non-clinical roles typically take 6 to 15 months. Your clinical years are an asset in paths like clinical liaison and utilization review.
Is it too late to leave SLP at 50?+
No. Mattie Murrey-Tegels rebuilt her career in her fifties after about 25 years as a medical SLP. At 50 the bigger questions are usually financial, like pension vesting and student-loan forgiveness, so work those out first.
Will I face age discrimination when I change careers?+
You might meet bias, but age discrimination against people 40 and older is illegal under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act, including in hiring, at employers with 20 or more employees. A résumé focused on recent, specific results makes it less likely to matter.
Will I lose my PSLF progress if I leave a school or hospital job?+
No. Qualifying payments you've already made still count, and the payments don't have to be consecutive. Payments made while you work for a for-profit employer don't count, so your progress pauses until you return to a qualifying employer.
Do I have to start at entry level if I leave SLP mid-career?+
Not always. Clinical liaison, utilization review, rehab leadership and clinical education all pay for clinical judgment. Paths like software engineering or UX research more often start at entry level.
