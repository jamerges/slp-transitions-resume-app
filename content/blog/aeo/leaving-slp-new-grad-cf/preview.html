Leaving SLP as a New Grad or During Your CF

The short answer

You can, but finishing the CF usually costs you less than a year and keeps the way back open, so most SLPs are better off finishing it first. If the problem is this placement, switching CF sites often fixes more than leaving the field. If you do leave, new grads tend to start in customer success, sales, marketing or operations.

What you'll learn

  • Finish the CF if you can. It's 36 weeks and 1,260 hours, and the CCC keeps every door open.
  • Try a new placement before a new field. Many bad CFs are bad placements.
  • You can go part-time. The CF just has to be finished within 48 months of starting.
  • A lapsed CCC can be reinstated, with 10 to 30 professional development hours and a fee.
  • New grads usually start in customer success, sales, marketing, HR or operations.

If you're a few months into your CF and already dreading Monday, you're not the first. One of the threads in our research is titled, word for word, "Has anyone ever left the field during their CFY & never looked back?" The replies split between "finish your C's" and "I left and never regretted it". Both camps are right about something.

Definition

Clinical Fellowship (CF): the supervised first stretch of paid work you need for ASHA's Certificate of Clinical Competence (CCC-SLP). ASHA requires at least 36 weeks and 1,260 hours, at least 80% of it direct clinical contact, finished within 48 months of starting.

Should I finish my CF before leaving?

Usually, yes, if you can stand it. ASHA's Clinical Fellowship guide sets the minimum at 36 weeks and 1,260 hours, with at least 80% in direct clinical contact. If you're already partway through, what's left may be a few months.

The CCC is the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy. With it, you can come back to clinical work, pick up PRN or teletherapy shifts while you switch fields, or take a non-clinical job that asks for a licensed clinician. Several SLPs in our research call their credentials a backup plan or a fishing license. You may never need it, and it's much harder to get later.

There's also a clock. ASHA says the CF must be finished within 48 months of when it started, and you can do it part-time. That means you can step back to part-time hours while you explore something else and still complete it.

When finishing isn't worth it:

  • Your health is suffering. Panic attacks, not sleeping, dreading every day. No credential is worth that.
  • You know you won't come back, and the job you're moving to doesn't care about the CCC.
  • The placement is unsafe or unethical, and you can't find another one.

Can I switch CF placements instead of quitting?

Often, yes, and it's worth trying first. A bad CF is frequently a bad placement: a caseload of 90, a mentor you barely see, a district that uses guilt to keep people. One CF in our research described being guilt-tripped by a district for leaving a 92-student caseload.

You don't have to finish the CF with the employer where you started it. Each stretch has to meet ASHA's supervision requirements, so before you move, check how the hours you've already done get reported and confirm your new mentor holds the CCC. Switching from schools to a clinic, or from a SNF to outpatient, can feel like a different job, because it is.

What happens to my CCC and license if I leave?

They don't disappear the day you walk out. Keeping the CCC means paying ASHA's annual fees and completing 30 professional development hours in each three-year maintenance interval. Your state license is separate and has its own renewal rules, so check with your state board.

If you let the CCC lapse, ASHA offers reinstatement: 10 professional development hours if it's been a year or less, 20 if one to two years, 30 if longer, plus a fee. So letting it go isn't permanent, but it's easier to keep it current if you think you might ever want it back.

What jobs can a new grad SLP get outside clinical work?

The ones that value how you talk to people and learn fast, more than years of clinical practice:

  • Customer success and implementation at teletherapy, ed-tech and health-tech companies. You understand the users because you were one.
  • Sales and business development, especially for products sold to schools, clinics and hospitals.
  • Marketing and content, where your ability to explain hard things simply counts. One SLP left after four years, at 28, for an entry-level SEO analyst job at a healthcare marketing company.
  • Recruiting and HR, where reading people and handling hard conversations are the job.
  • Operations and project coordination, which reward the scheduling and paperwork you already juggle.

Paths that sell clinical judgment, like utilization review or clinical liaison, often want a few years of clinical experience. That's one more reason finishing the CF, and maybe a year after it, can open doors you'd otherwise wait longer for.

What about my student loans?

If you're relying on Public Service Loan Forgiveness, check the employer first. Only government agencies and 501(c)(3) nonprofits qualify. Many school districts and nonprofit hospitals do, and most private companies don't. The good news is that the 120 qualifying payments don't have to be consecutive, so a few years at a for-profit company pauses your progress without erasing it.

Am I wasting my degree?

No. You spent six years learning how people communicate, how to assess a problem, how to plan around it and how to explain it to worried families. That goes with you into every path on our map of 20 careers.

Frequently asked questions

Can I quit my CF and still become an SLP later?+
Yes, but you'll need to complete a CF to earn the CCC. ASHA requires at least 36 weeks and 1,260 hours, finished within 48 months of when the fellowship started. Check with ASHA about how earlier hours count if you stop and restart.
Is it a mistake to leave SLP right after grad school?+
Not necessarily, but finishing the CF first usually costs less than a year and keeps the option to return. If the problem is your placement rather than the work, switching CF sites is often the better first step.
What non-clinical jobs can a new grad SLP get?+
Customer success, implementation, sales, marketing, recruiting, HR and operations roles are the usual entry points. Roles built on clinical judgment, like utilization review or clinical liaison, often want a few years of clinical experience.
Can I do my CF part-time?+
Yes. ASHA allows the CF to be completed part-time, as long as you work at least 5 hours a week and meet the 1,260-hour minimum within 48 months of starting.
What happens to my CCC if I leave the field?+
To keep it, you pay ASHA's annual fees and complete 30 professional development hours every three years. If it lapses, ASHA allows reinstatement with 10 to 30 hours depending on how long it's been, plus a fee.
