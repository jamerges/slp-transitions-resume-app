# An SLP Who Became a Health Care Facility Surveyor

**The short answer:** After more than a decade as an SLP in skilled nursing and acute care, including time as an assistant director of rehab, this SLP was hired as a health care facility surveyor for a state health department. They took no extra courses. The search took twelve to eighteen months, five applications and two interviews, both for the same job. The first interview ended in a no. The second time the job was posted, the hiring team called and asked them to apply again.

This path isn't on our list of twenty non-clinical careers. After reading their answers, I think it should be.

**At a glance**

- **Before:** SLP in SNF / long-term care and acute hospital settings, 11 to 20 years, including time as assistant director of rehab
- **Now:** Health care facility surveyor for a state health department, hybrid, started August 2026 and still in training
- **Search:** 12 to 18 months, 5 applications, 2 interviews
- **How it came:** applied cold on the state's careers site, then was invited to reapply
- **Extra education:** none

## Why did they start looking?

There was no single moment. "The daily burnout of being 'on' all day was wearing on me, and I started to dread the day to day."

What they noticed about themselves mattered more than what they wanted to escape: "I realized what I enjoyed was more documentation and administrative-type tasks."

## What does a health care facility surveyor's week look like?

Right now it's mostly training. "I'm currently still in training to be a state surveyor, so I'm doing a lot of learning modules from home. I do attend classes at the main office, usually once per week."

Once trained, the job moves into the field: "I will be traveling to different facilities within my region for surveys, and there will be periodic days working from home to write up reports."

## How did the job actually come to them?

Through the front door, twice. "I applied a few times on my state's career website. I finally got an interview in October 2025, but ended up not getting the job."

The no didn't end it. "They posted the same position again in May 2026 (they were on a hiring spree), and called me to say that if I was still interested I should re-apply, which I did. I interviewed again and got the job that time."

## What changed on the résumé?

Nothing. "I didn't change anything on my resume, my clinical background was what was necessary for this role, despite being a non-clinical role."

What did change was the experience on it. "I think what did help and ultimately got me my first interview after applying a few times (for this same job, over several years) was that I took a position as an assistant director of rehab. That administrative/managerial experience was extremely beneficial."

## Did they need more education?

No. "They take clinical backgrounds of many kinds, including social workers, physical therapists, occupational therapists, dietitians, and nurses. Training is on the job and EXTENSIVE."

## Which clinical skills mattered most?

In their words:

- "General medical awareness"
- "Awareness of SNF processes including infection control and kitchen processes"
- "My experience with IDT (such as Risk meetings as an ADOR)"
- "Experience with past surveys at my own facility"

That last one is worth sitting with. If you've worked in a SNF, you have probably lived through a survey from the other side of the clipboard.

## What was harder than expected?

The chair. "I am not used to sitting for extended periods, and it tends to hurt my back (getting old!). In the SNF I was up and down every few minutes, now I have to make a concerted effort to move."

## What would they tell an SLP sitting where they used to sit?

"Ideally, have a direction on where you'd like to go outside of speech that isn't just 'get out of speech' and what your ideal work day would look like. That helps to narrow down what you'd like or not like to do. In my case, I do enjoy the medical field but no longer wanted direct patient care. The state surveyor position is a perfect combination of those [two] big features."

## What to take from this

- **A no can be the first half of a yes.** The first interview put them in front of the hiring team, and when the job reopened, the team called them. If a role you want gets reposted, apply again.
- **The bridge was a step into management.** An assistant director of rehab role added the administrative experience the surveyor job looked for. If a lead or management role comes up in your setting, it may be the most useful line you can add.
