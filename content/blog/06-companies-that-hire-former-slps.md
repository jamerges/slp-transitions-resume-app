Here's a mistake I watch burnt-out SLPs make every single week: they open Indeed, type "non-clinical SLP jobs," scroll through three pages of nothing, and conclude nobody wants them.

Nobody wants them *on Indeed*. That's different.

The companies that hire former SLPs mostly aren't posting jobs labeled "former SLP wanted." They're speech-tech companies who need people who actually understand AAC. Health systems that need clinicians who can talk to clinicians. Edtech and health-tech companies that need someone who can walk a stressed, skeptical human through a complicated product — which, if you think about it, was your Tuesday.

So this post flips the search. Instead of job-first, we're going company-first: the categories of companies that reliably hire people like us, what the roles pay, and the entry doors that actually open. Then the part most lists skip — how to actually get in.

## Why does company-first beat job-board-first?

Three unromantic, decisive reasons:

**1. Referrals decide most successful transitions.** The single most consistent finding across real SLP career changes: generic online applications through Indeed and LinkedIn without referrals is the approach that *fails*. A referral requires knowing which companies you're targeting before a job is even posted. Company-first is how referrals become possible.

**2. Speed decides who gets read.** Only applications submitted in the first 1–2 days of a posting get real attention — the average opening now draws around 242 applications, up 111% since 2022. If you're browsing job boards, you're finding week-old postings. If you're watching ten target companies with alerts on, you're applying within 48 hours.

**3. Targeting beats volume.** One real transition: 11 months, 113 *tailored* applications, 7 interviews, 1 offer. Tailoring is only possible when you understand the company. A short list you know deeply outperforms a long list you're spraying.

Okay. The categories.

## Which speech and AAC tech companies hire former SLPs?

This is the warmest water in the entire job market for us, because it's the one place your CCC-SLP is itself the credential. These companies build for our clients, sell to our colleagues, and need people who speak both languages.

**Named companies:** Expressable, Presence, Tobii Dynavox, PRC-Saltillo, Lingraphica, Passy-Muir.

- **What roles:** Clinical educator / learning consultant (training clinicians and families on devices), customer success, implementation and onboarding, and clinically-informed marketing and content.
- **What they pay:** Tobii Dynavox's "Learning Consultant" role runs **$78–116k** (per Glassdoor). Customer success and implementation roles across health-tech generally land in the **$75–120k** range.
- **How to get in:** Watch these companies specifically — set alerts, follow their people on LinkedIn. Entry doors are the clinical educator and implementation roles, where they're explicitly hiring your clinical expertise rather than asking you to hide it. One line that lands in these interviews: "I've been doing customer success my whole career — I just called it patient/family care" — followed by a metric.

## Which health systems hire SLPs for non-clinical roles?

You don't have to leave healthcare to leave the treatment room. Large rehab and health systems hire clinicians into roles where the credential is the qualification.

**Named employers:** Encompass Health, Select Medical, Lifepoint.

- **What roles:** **Clinical/rehab liaison** — called the #1 non-clinical title for rehab professionals — pays **$84k–135k**, and your clinical credential is sufficient to apply. Also **utilization review** at **$80–88k** ($37–62/hr), which skews heavily remote and is one of the fastest entries out there. State **Disability Determination Services** roles are a related door — with honest tradeoffs: one SLP took a $13k pay cut for the mobility and PSLF eligibility. Tradeoffs are real; sometimes they're worth it.
- **How to get in:** These are the fastest-runway options on this page because there's no retraining story to tell. Your job is translation, not transformation: your chart auditing is detail-oriented QA, your justifications to insurance are utilization review experience you already have.

## Which edtech and health-tech companies hire SLPs into customer success?

Beyond the speech-specific companies, the wider edtech and health-tech world hires former clinicians into customer success, implementation, and training constantly — it's the best effort-to-odds ratio of any transition path. These companies need people who can teach complicated things to overwhelmed users, manage long-term relationships, and handle objections without flinching. You have a decade of reps.

- **What roles:** Implementation specialist, onboarding manager, customer success manager, customer education / training.
- **What they pay:** Roughly **$75–120k**. Real outcome: a medical SLP went through SV Academy's free customer success bootcamp with a paid internship and landed a remote SaaS manager role at **$115–120k**.
- **How to get in:** The entry doors are **implementation specialist and onboarding manager** — aim there before CSM. Your translation: families become customers (long-term relationship management, retention), selling treatment plans to skeptical families becomes persuasion and objection handling, translating jargon for parents becomes plain-language communication.

## Where do healthcare data and analytics roles live?

Quietly one of the most successful SLP landing zones — with a targeting rule most people get backwards.

- **What roles:** Healthcare data analyst — falls data, discharge follow-up, outcomes reporting inside healthcare organizations.
- **What they pay:** Real outcomes: one former SLP is now a fully remote analyst earning more than her SLP salary; another reports **"$20k more a year base than I ever made as an SLP."**
- **How to get in:** Target **healthcare orgs, NOT big tech.** Hospitals and health organizations value someone who understands the clinical context behind the numbers; big tech will make you compete with CS grads on their turf. The Google Data Analytics certificate or a DataCamp route covers the skills — then your clinical background becomes the differentiator instead of the liability. Fair warning on timeline: one analyst's search took 500+ applications over 6 months. It's a real path, not a fast one.

## What about clinical informatics and EHR companies?

The role where your most annoying skill — being the EMR superuser everyone bugged for help — becomes the résumé.

- **What roles:** EHR/Epic application analyst, clinical informatics analyst, go-live support.
- **What they pay:** **$97.8k–154k.**
- **How to get in:** Critical fact that saves people months: **Epic certification cannot be self-obtained** — it's employer-sponsored only. So don't chase the cert; chase the sponsor. The real doors: sponsor-track junior analyst roles at health systems, go-live/activation support gigs, and Epic consulting firms like **Nordic and Tegria**. The self-serve credential that *does* exist: CAHIMS from HIMSS, designed for career changers with no experience prerequisite. Your ammunition: EMR superuser work, template building, documentation-workflow expertise. One real SLP made exactly this jump to Epic Application Analyst.

## What other role types should be on your list?

Quick tour of the remaining categories where SLPs demonstrably land:

- **Project/program management** — the single most-traveled road out, with documented six-figure outcomes (one IT PM hit six figures 15 months in). Nearly every company category above hires PMs, which is why it pairs well with a company-first search.
- **Marketing, copywriting, and content** — **$80k–141k** among documented transitions. One freelancer started with a single $200 Upwork article and now runs a micro-agency; another landed corporate marketing after about a year of applications and noted: "I actually got a raise and bonus this year, whereas in my 5 years in healthcare I was given a .35 cent raise."
- **Instructional design** — portfolio-first, no degree or certificate required: 3–5 samples in Articulate Storyline 360/Rise plus process docs, spec work fully acceptable. Typical timeline 6–12 months.

One honest anti-recommendation, because a real list tells you what to skip: **pharma MSL roles are effectively doctorate-gated** (PhD/PharmD/MD). Don't spend months chasing it — clinical/rehab liaison is the accessible cousin. And UX research is heavily oversaturated right now (35% more grads in five years against flat openings); it's not impossible, but it shouldn't be your Plan A.

## How do you actually get in?

A list of companies is a map, not a vehicle. Three moves turn it into interviews:

**1. Run the referral playbook.** Pick 10–15 target companies from the categories above. Find their people — especially former clinicians — on LinkedIn and ask for 20-minute informational chats. Brace yourself: about 20–25% of these asks get no response, and that's completely normal. Send them anyway. Since referrals decide most successful transitions, this unglamorous step is the highest-value hour in your week. Every conversation compounds: intel, warmth, and eventually the internal referral that skips your resume past the pile.

**2. Apply in the first 48 hours.** Set alerts on your target companies. When the role posts, you move within 1–2 days — because those are the only applications that get seen before the crowd arrives. This is the hidden advantage of company-first: you literally cannot do this from a job board.

**3. Translate before you apply.** Your resume in clinical language is invisible to these employers — recruiters spend about 7.4 seconds on a first pass, and "caseload of 60 students with IEPs" doesn't survive it. "Managed a portfolio of 60 concurrent clients with individual goals, timelines, and documented outcomes" does. Keep your numbers, mirror the job description's exact vocabulary, and lead with a value summary, not an objective (objective statements get ~28% fewer callbacks). Companies want numbers: by how much did your project improve which variable?
