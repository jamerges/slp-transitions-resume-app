# LinkedIn playbook — built from Hannah Pugh's feed (Aug 2026)

Source: ~65 posts from linkedin.com/in/pughhann (Practitioner Pivot, OT →
health-tech client success, ~3.5k LI followers / ~35k IG). Engagement numbers
below are hers, used as evidence for what the clinician-transition audience
rewards on LinkedIn.

## What her data shows

| Format | Her results | Verdict for James |
|---|---|---|
| Time-anchored personal reflection ("Five years ago today…") | 90–300 reactions — her entire top tier | Adopt. James has the arc and has never posted it. |
| Community meta-commentary (gatekeeping, networking etiquette) | 60–140 | Adopt sparingly — needs a take, not vibes. |
| Numbered day-series ("Day 1 of 5: my job search") | 33–117; the day with real numbers (8 resumes → 4 interviews → 2 offers → 1 role) beat the advice-only days | Adopt, powered by the 12 interviews. |
| Identity thesis ("I'm an OT working in health tech", not "I used to be an OT") | 44–48 with heavy comments — her intellectual territory | Adopt the SLP version; it's also our product thesis. |
| Peer tagging (naming 4–7 colleagues) | 56–102, plus their networks | Adopt — 12 published guests are a ready roster. |
| Anything that sells (webinars, events, products) | 2–31, chronically | **Don't sell in the feed.** Sell in the profile, the funnel, the list. |
| Question CTA at the end of nearly every post | Drives her comment counts | Adopt. |

Her exploitable gap: almost no hard data. Salary, timelines, funnels are
absent or anecdotal, and the voice is emoji/hashtag-heavy. Our style guide's
restraint + research-facts.md specificity is the differentiation — same warmth,
receipts attached.

Her actual revenue motion (visible across the feed): co-hosted paid webinars
($25–50, capped ~50 seats) with adjacent creators — Katie Huggins (CPRW,
resumes), Lauren Curtis (Hey Health Tech). The cross-promo web of 3–4 adjacent
creators is the machine; the feed just keeps it warm.

## Post batch (hooks drafted in James's voice — style-guide compliant)

1. **Identity thesis.** "There are more SLPs working in tech than anyone
   realizes. Implementation. Customer success. Clinical informatics. UX
   research. But somewhere in the move, we stop saying 'I'm an SLP.' I think
   that's backwards — the license is why they were hired." End: "If you're an
   SLP working outside the clinic, I'd genuinely like to hear what you do."
   (Comments double as interview sourcing and VoC.)
2. **James's own time-anchored arc.** "X years ago I wrote my last SOAP note.
   Psych undergrad → SLP → copywriter at a mental-health-tech company. The
   detour is the résumé." Her two 300-reaction posts are this exact shape.
3. **Day-series from the interviews.** "5 SLPs, 5 days, 5 different exits."
   One guest per day, each with the concrete numbers (starting salary band,
   months from decision to offer, what actually got the interview). Tag each
   guest their day.
4. **Funnel-numbers post.** The $200-article-to-agency story, or any guest
   with a countable application funnel. Numbers-in-hook, story-in-body.
5. **Salary-band cards as image posts.** The sizzle-reel stat frames
   (liaison $84–135k / 1–3 months; UR $80–88k; CS $75–120k) reformatted
   1200×1500. Three posts, not one.
6. **Contrarian data read.** Her highest-comment analytical post questioned
   LinkedIn's "PT = #3 fastest-growing job" list (17 comments). Ours: what
   the BLS "growing profession" number hides about why SLPs still leave —
   caseloads, productivity requirements, the license ceiling.
7. **The guilt post.** Her "who will be left to treat?" pulled 13 comments.
   We have the researched version: should-you-quit-slp (blog 3397).
   Repurpose as a text post ending on the question, link in comments.
8. **Bad-advice correction.** "The most common bad advice SLPs get about
   clinical informatics: 'just get Epic certified.' You can't — Epic
   certification is employer-sponsored. Here's the door that's actually open."
   Factual-guardrail content nobody else in the niche runs.
9. **Translation pairs.** "Caseload of 60 → portfolio of 60 concurrent
   clients." The retired homepage strip is a carousel; pairs live in
   content/blog/03 + build_home.py TRANSLATIONS.
10. **AOTA-equivalent visibility.** When guests speak (Levy/Seaver, ASHA
    Centennial), post as their interviewer, tag them.

Rules carried over: end on a question; tag real people; no hashtag walls
(3 max); links in comments, not body; never pitch the products in the post —
profile and featured section do that job.
