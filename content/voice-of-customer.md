# Voice of the Customer — how SLPs actually talk about leaving

Purpose: keep every piece of copy (blog, email, social, landing page, app) in the
prospect's own language instead of marketer language. Pair with `style-guide.md`
(James's voice) — this file is the *reader's* voice; the style guide is the *writer's*.

**Status of each entry is marked.** ✅ = verified verbatim quote with a source.
⚠️ = recurring pattern observed across communities; safe to *echo the theme*, do not
present as a quote. This file is living — grow it (see "Growing this corpus").

---

## 1. The emotional sequence (map copy to the stage, not past it)

1. **Private doubt** — they haven't told anyone. Searches at 11pm.
2. **Guilt/identity crisis** — the degree, the loans, "who am I if not an SLP?"
3. **Permission-seeking** — lurking in groups, reading exit stories, asking "has anyone actually done this?"
4. **Practical panic** — "what else can I even do? I don't want to start over."
5. **Action** — resume, LinkedIn, applications, interviews.

The quiz sells to stages 2–3. The $9 report sells to stage 3–4. The $24 suite sells to stage 5.
**Copy that talks stage-5 language (ATS, keywords, interviews) to a stage-2 reader loses her.**

## 3. Recurring themes ⚠️ (echo the theme; never fabricate a quote)

- **Productivity & documentation**: unpaid documentation time, productivity
  percentages, sessions billed back-to-back. The villain is the *system*, per brand.
- **The car cry**: crying in the car before/after work is a recurring confession in
  SLP burnout threads. Use with care — name it at most once per piece; it's powerful
  and easily cheapened.
- **Degree grief**: "three degrees / six years / the loans" against a $60-70k salary.
  Loss-aversion language: "throwing away my degree," "wasting my CCCs."
- **Identity fusion**: "being an SLP is who I am." Exit copy must offer identity
  *continuity* ("you're still the person who explains hard things simply") — never
  "reinvent yourself."
- **Permission hunger**: the most-upvoted exit posts are plain testimony: "I left. Here's
  what I do now. AMA." The *format itself* is the value — proof it's survivable.
- **Fear of the bottom rung**: "I can't afford entry-level pay" / "I don't want to
  start over at 35." Copy must attack the false binary (lateral moves exist; CS/liaison
  roles pay ≥ clinical).
- **Skepticism of coaches**: burned by $2k courses and "just believe in yourself"
  coaching. Price transparency, refunds, and "here's exactly what you get" convert;
  hype repels. (Lauren Braley's "ungatekeep" framing works for the same reason.)

## 4. Words they use vs words marketers use

| They say | Marketers say (avoid) |
|---|---|
| "I want out" / "leave the field" | "pivot journey," "career evolution" |
| "burnt out," "done," "drained" | "seeking new challenges" |
| "non-clinical" (their actual search term) | "alternative careers" (they use this too, but less) |
| "what else can I even do?" | "unlock your potential" |
| "scared to start over" | "embrace change" |
| "the paperwork/productivity" | "workplace challenges" |
| "my CCCs," "the degree," "grad school loans" | "your credentials" |
| "remote job," "work from home" | "flexible lifestyle" |
| "salary," exact numbers | "compensation," vague "six figures" |
| "has anyone actually done this?" | "success stories" |

**Banned in all copy** (marketer-tells): unlock, empower, level up, dream job,
girlboss/bossbabe, "the universe," journey (as noun for the transition), thrive
(as imperative), "you've got this" as a close. Also everything in the app's
banned-AI-words list (lib/anthropic.ts) applies to marketing copy.

**Use sparingly, they're load-bearing**: "trapped," "guilt," "permission," "I want out."
Once per piece, max — mirroring pain is validation; repeating it is wallowing.

## 5. Register rules

1. **Validate the push, sell the pull.** Open in their pain words; move to what they're
   moving *toward* within 2-3 paragraphs. (Matches the app's push→pull reframe rule.)
2. **Numbers are respect.** Exact salaries, exact timelines, exact costs. Rounded-up
   vagueness reads as coaching-industry sleaze to this audience.
3. **Never bash ASHA/CCCs** (brand rule) even though parts of the community do. The
   villain is workload, productivity math, and admin — not the profession's bodies.
4. **"Setting change" is a tell.** Anyone who's ruled it out is past stage 2. Name the
   deflection; don't perform it.
5. **Proof beats promise.** A single named transition ("school SLP → CS at an AAC
   company, 4 months") outsells any adjective.

## 6. Growing this corpus

James is in the Facebook groups (SLPs Out of the Box, SLP Transitions-adjacent groups,
~20k-member exit communities) that Claude cannot access. To grow this file:
- Screenshot or paste high-engagement posts/comments (strip names) into a chat and ask
  to fold them in — the `/voice-of-customer` skill knows this file's format.
- Prioritize: posts with 50+ comments, first-person exit stories, and the *questions*
  asked under them (questions = objections = copy angles).
- Quiz free-text (`whyLeaving` field in the $9 intake) is VoC gold — periodically
  export a sample and fold in themes (never verbatim customer data into public copy).
