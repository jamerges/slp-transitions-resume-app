# SLP Transitions — Writing Style Guide

Source: slptransitions.com, studied 2026-07-30. Author/founder: James Berges — an SLP with 3+ years clinical experience, now part-time private practice + freelance marketing.

## 1. Voice & Persona
James speaks in first person — a peer who is one step ahead, "the big sibling who left the hospital job first and is reporting back." Not a guru, not an HR consultant.
- Cites his own pain honestly: "While I enjoy connecting 1-1 with students and clients, the red-tape, never-ending paperwork, and uncontrollable caseloads made me start looking at career paths outside of SLP."
- Frames the site as service, not authority: "By sharing these stories, I want to help other SLPs get started on the right foot."
- Offers direct access: "reach out personally at james@slptransitions.com"
- ~Half the content is interviews: James is host/curator, the transitioned SLP is the hero. His voice appears in framing, subheads, and "Takeaways."
- Reader: burnt-out SLP who feels trapped but guilty about wanting out. Core reframe: **"You're more than just a clinician."**

## 2. Tone Attributes
1. **Empathetic-direct** (validate, then push): "What you actually need is specific information about what's out there, what it takes to get there, and what people are earning—so you can make a real decision instead of spinning in uncertainty."
2. **Anti-toxic-positivity / anti-vagueness**: mocks both extremes — "either too vague ('explore your options!') or too discouraging ('you'll have to start over')."
3. **Psychology-literate, plain language**: "The Sunk Cost bias says that we tend to over-value situations we've already invested time into—even if we know there's a better option out there." Imposter syndrome is "a) annoying but normal. b) just trying to protect you."
4. **Self-deprecating, whimsical humor**: "I was the kid in Yogurtland who couldn't just keep it simple with two complementary flavors… Topping tradeoffs be damned." Alliterative personas ("Wall Street Wally," "SLP Susan"); "my cat's grain-free pate budget."
5. **Earnest, permission-granting closes**: "I deserve to wake up and enjoy going to work. Everyone deserves that." — vulnerability lands un-joked.
6. **Pragmatic optimism**: "Don't expect to start a new career path and instantly have a 6-figure salary, with autonomy, flexibility, impact, creativity, and meaning."

## 3. Rhythm
- 1–3 sentence paragraphs. One-line fragments for punch: "Enter: The goldilocks stress zone." / "The breaking point."
- Alternate reflective anecdote → directive action step. Rhetorical questions ("Is this you?").
- Em-dashes, parentheticals, italics for inner voice. Polish level = "smart friend's email," not magazine copy. Don't over-lacquer.

## 4. Vocabulary
- **Recurring**: "fulfilling work" (the #1 brand phrase), "non-clinical," "burnout," "transition," "transferable skills," "flexibility," "flow state," "make the leap," "Sunday night scaries," "mental models," "own my time," "clinicians love."
- **Reader address**: "you," "fellow SLPs," "clinicians." Never "ladies," never clinical-formal.
- **Burnout**: systemic, not personal weakness — "red-tape, never-ending paperwork, and uncontrollable caseloads." "There were days when I felt like a customer service representative; a customer service representative with three degrees."
- **Money**: open + specific ("SLP to 96K"), salary transparency as virtue, but ranked below "mental health and quality of life."
- **ASHA/CCC: absent.** Never bash the professional bodies — the villain is workload/system conditions. Do NOT import Reddit-style ASHA vitriol.
- **Avoided**: corporate jargon, hustle/girlboss language, shame, false promises.

## 5. Structure
- **Openers**: (a) compressed hero summary ("Short Summary: An SLP working part-time gigs in SNFs taught herself to code. Now she makes over 96K…"), (b) personal confession/anecdote, or (c) community data ("I recently asked the SLP Alternatives Facebook group… 71 comments later, some themes emerged:").
- **Interview template**: backstory Q → "What do you do now?" → skills-transfer Q → pros/cons → "What advice do you have for fellow SLPs…" — with narrative subheads, ends with bulleted **Takeaways** + thank-you.
- **Formatting**: heavy bold on key insights, bullets, question-format H2s, sparing emoji.
- **Closers**: warm, low-pressure — "Thanks for reading! If you found value in this article, then you'll love the SLP Transitions newsletter." Or "email me."

## 8. Absent (keep absent)
No ASHA/CCC criticism. No urgency countdowns or income-scream marketing. No therapy/clinical content. No "stay and cope" self-care framing — always exit/expansion. No political/insurance commentary. No AI-content sheen — keep human texture.

## 8a. No table-setting sentences (added 2026-08-01, James's correction)
Cut sentences whose only job is to announce the significance of the sentence next to it — "That ordering matters." / "This is the line worth writing down." / "Here's the part that matters." / "Notice what she did." These are a tell: they tell the reader how to feel about a fact instead of trusting the fact to land on its own. Let the substance do the talking — state the fact, quote, or turn, and move on without narrating it.

Also vary sentence rhythm more aggressively than a stock "short punchy sentence after a long one" pattern — that itself becomes a tic if every section does it. Pull actual technique from prose craft: reorder a sentence so the most vivid word lands last ("The small boys came early to the hanging," not "...to the hanging early"); cut adverbs and reach for the stronger verb instead; let some sentences run long and specific, not just short and declarative. Read a paragraph aloud before it ships — if every sentence has the same shape, rewrite one.
