# The Teacher Career Coach Course: audit (2026-09-18)

Read as a paying student through James's account. Notes are summaries in my
words; nothing here is her material verbatim, and her licence page forbids
reusing it to coach or to build a course, so this file is for positioning and
product decisions only.

**What I could and could not read.** Every lesson page, every text block,
every link and download list. Captions existed on only five videos (Start
Here, 1.2, 3.1, 3.2 and the remote-work bonus), so those are covered in depth.
The other 26 videos, about 300 minutes, have no captions and no transcript.
The workbook PDF (editable, "tcc-all-mods-editable-2022-12-22"), the "Former
Teacher Salary & Job Titles" PDF and the eight example résumés are downloads I
did not fetch. They are the text version of the video lessons and worth
reading if James wants the rest.

## Shape of the product

- Platform: SamCart Courses. 5 modules, 31 videos (~400 min), 42 lesson pages,
  2 files, 0 quizzes, 0 worksheets. Progress bar and "Completed" ticks; no XP,
  streaks or badges.
- Everything is video-first. Text under each video is short: a reminder to use
  the workbook, one or two links, occasionally a real resource block.
- Dated 2022 in most places (workbook filename, résumé examples, "distance
  learning" bonus). Some 2024/2025 additions: the AI clarity bonus, the remote
  bonus, the salary PDF (Nov 2025).
- Monetisation around the course: 1:1 coaching calls linked at the top of the
  dashboard; Coursera/Udemy affiliate links on the upskilling page; SheCodes
  20% partner link; Orchard (career exploration app) free for a year with
  purchase; the podcast cross-linked from most lessons.

## What the captioned lessons actually teach

**1.2 Clarity (18 min).** Name your why (hours, balance, salary, culture).
List what you would sacrifice (location, commute, a dip in pay for the right
door) and your non-negotiables (benefits, leave, retirement). Do the hourly
math: a $50k salary at 55 hours a week is $17.50 an hour, so a $48k job at 40
hours pays more. Her survey: 28% of her students took a pay cut, 98% of those
do not regret it; newer teachers usually earn more immediately. Ends with a
commitment sentence to write in a planner. Personal story: she left at rock
bottom, depression and stress illness, and the next job "seemed like a scam"
because it was too good.

**3.1 Résumé (20 min).** Three mistakes: not enough credit, teacher lingo,
no posting keywords. Job duty vs accomplishment ("teaches sixth grade" vs
"built a 12-week programme for 29 participants in a month"). Keep a career
accomplishments journal and pull from it per application. Honest title
adjustments only (learning consultant to corporate trainer, never to
director). "Educator transitioning to X" as the headline. Translate students
to participants or clients, parents to stakeholders, grade level to
department. The one genuinely clever move: if a posting wants CSAT or KPI
experience, send families a one-question satisfaction survey now, track it,
and you own a truthful metric before you leave. Ends with the emotional
reality (you will cry over the draft) and "it only takes one yes".

**3.2 ATS (26 min).** Keywords plus acronyms, core competencies two or three
times, chronological format only, one and a half to two pages, PDF unless
told otherwise, no colour, charts, Canva or templates. Section order: header,
summary in the target title, experience, community leadership, education
(no dates), technology. Keep three role-specific base résumés. Red-flag
wording: "position eliminated", never "fired" or "left for growth". Age
bias: list 10 to 15 years only. Gaps: older than five years and under two,
ignore; inside five years and over eight months, fill with volunteering. Apply
at a 70% match. A referral bypasses the scanner. A long warning about
Googling résumé advice: outdated, selling templates, or just opinion.

**Remote bonus (18 min).** Flexibility means different things; client-facing
remote roles expect you at a desk. Her survey of about 400 former teachers:
90% could not watch their children full time while working remotely. Most
remote jobs are location-restricted, so search USA, then state, then city.
Skip LinkedIn Easy Apply, go to the careers page, apply to one role per
company. Hiring managers fear two things: people gaming remote work and
people who cannot self-manage. Show tech self-sufficiency and self-starter
stories; a warm referral beats everything. LinkedIn venting is a red flag.

**5.1 text block.** Three HR "code phrases" for why you are leaving (direction
change, management change, no room to grow), each with the situation it
covers and a warning that "no room to grow" reads as flaky under three years.
Portfolio advice: build a fake customer-learning portal for a company that
does not exist. Six questions to ask the interviewer, including "any reason
you would not hire me?".

**FAQ highlights.** Nobody searches LinkedIn for "former teacher", they search
"customer success", so rebrand to the target title. Apply two months before
you can start. One or two applications a week means a year of searching;
fifteen to twenty is aggressive. No reply is normal; follow up weekly.

## Where she is stronger than us

1. **Proof from her own alumni.** Salary and title data, interview questions
   "former teachers were asked", survey percentages, spotlights. Every
   number comes from her students, which is unfalsifiable and personal. Our
   numbers come from Glassdoor and the BLS.
2. **Static example résumés by target role.** Eight PDFs a cold reader can
   open before paying.
3. **Community loop.** Circle group, badge on landing, alumni group, spotlight
   form. Stories feed the marketing and the marketing feeds the stories.
4. **Volume of encouragement.** "Hang in there" lessons close two modules;
   1.2 and 3.1 both end on the emotional cost. She names the crying.
5. **Concrete hacks.** The CSAT survey, the hourly-rate math, the three code
   phrases, the fake portal portfolio.

## Where we are stronger

1. Interactive lessons that carry answers forward (verdict, dials, energy
   audit, path map). Hers is watch-and-write-in-a-PDF.
2. Sourced numbers with the source under each lesson. Several of hers are
   unsourced ("75% of résumés never get seen", "300 to 600 applicants").
3. The stage map. She has one "clarity" lesson; we have five stages, a free
   verdict, and offers matched to stage.
4. Captions and text. Most of her videos have neither, so the course is
   unsearchable and inaccessible. Ours is read-and-do.
5. Guardrails. Her upskilling page is an affiliate shop; ours says when a
   certificate does not help and why Epic cannot be bought.
6. Currency. Much of hers is 2022.

## What to take, ranked

1. **A "questions former SLPs were asked" bank by path.** Ask every buyer at
   the Module 6 checkpoint and in the day-6 email; publish inside Module 6.
2. **An alumni salary and title sheet.** Add one optional question to the
   Module 7 "Tell your story" action (title, range, months). Publish the
   aggregate as proof on the $19 and OS pages once there are ten rows.
3. **Three example résumés by path** (liaison, customer success, UR) as a
   lead magnet and as proof on the Suite page. Real translations of a real
   anonymised SLP résumé, not invented.
4. **The CSAT hack, SLP version**, into Module 4 "Numbers you already have":
   a one-question caregiver or family satisfaction survey run for a month,
   anonymised, gives a truthful metric.
5. **Your real hourly rate** into Module 1 "What you can't afford to lose".
6. **A story spotlight form** on slptransitions.com, linked from the Module 7
   action and the Congratulations email.
7. **Telehealth and remote-clinical bullet bank** for the Suite's résumé
   guidance.
8. **A licence line** on the course dashboard.
9. Optional: the two-prompt AI clarity bonus, and the follow-up email scripts
   in Module 6.

## Not worth copying

The affiliate upskilling page, the Trello board, the "distance learning"
framing, video-only delivery, the SharePoint links, the one-on-one coaching
upsell at the top of the dashboard.
