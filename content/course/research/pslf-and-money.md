# The loan is a calendar: PSLF, the pay-cut budget, COBRA — research notes

Compiled 2026-09-04 for the Transition OS money lesson. Audience: U.S. SLPs deciding whether and when to leave clinical employment. Every factual claim below carries a URL and access date. Where a rule is unsettled or I could not verify it from a primary source, it is flagged and repeated in the hedge list at the end. Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice.

## 1. PSLF: the mechanics

The program lives in federal regulation at 34 CFR 685.219. All citations in this section are to the current eCFR text (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-685.219, accessed 2026-09-04), which carries the source note [91 FR 23897, May 1, 2026].

**Who counts as a qualifying employer.** The regulation lists: a U.S.-based federal, state, local or Tribal government organization, agency or entity; a public child or family service agency; an organization exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code; a Tribal college or university; and a nonprofit that provides a "non-governmental public service" and is not organized for profit, not a labor union and not a partisan political organization (§ 685.219(b)(27)). A for-profit company does not qualify, and neither does a private practice organized for profit. "Employee" means someone the organization issues a W-2 to, or who is W-2'd through a payroll service under contract to the qualifying employer, or a contracted employee in a role that state law bars the employer from filling directly (§ 685.219(b)(9)). A 1099 contractor working for a nonprofit is not an employee of it.

**Full time is 30 hours, and you may add jobs together.** Full-time means "working in qualifying employment in one or more jobs" a minimum average of 30 hours per week during the period being certified (§ 685.219(b)(11)(i)(A)). Two part-time nonprofit jobs at 15 hours each qualify; 25 hours at a nonprofit plus 15 at a for-profit does not, because only qualifying employment counts toward the 30.

**120 payments, and employment on both ends.** You need the equivalent of 120 monthly qualifying payments made after October 1, 2007, on Direct Loans; you must be employed full-time by a qualifying employer both when the 120th payment is satisfied and when you apply for forgiveness (§ 685.219(c)(1)). A month is credited only if you were employed full-time by a qualifying employer "at any point during the month" (§ 685.219(c)(2)(vi)). The payments do not have to be consecutive.

**Qualifying repayment plans** are the income-driven plans under § 685.209, the 10-year standard plan, any plan whose payment is at least the 10-year standard amount, the Repayment Assistance Plan, and ICR only for payments received on or before June 30, 2028 (§ 685.219(b)(28)).

**Certifying employment.** Federal Student Aid's guidance is to certify employment annually and again whenever you change employers or employment status (studentaid.gov guidance summarized at https://studentaid.gov/articles/manage-your-pslf-progress/ and https://studentaid.gov/pslf/, accessed 2026-09-04 — see hedge list; studentaid.gov renders client-side and I could not pull the sentence verbatim). The regulation itself requires certification at application (§ 685.219(e)(3)) rather than annually, so annual filing is best practice, not a legal deadline.

**Leaving for a non-qualifying employer.** Nothing in § 685.219 removes credit already earned. Months worked for a non-qualifying employer simply are not credited, so the count pauses rather than resets. The consequence that matters is § 685.219(c)(1)(ii): you must be back in qualifying full-time employment at the moment of the 120th payment and at application. A three-year detour into a for-profit health-tech job costs you three years of progress, not the eight you already had.

**Buyback exists in regulation.** For months when you were employed full-time by a qualifying employer but were in a deferment or forbearance that is not on the credited list, you may obtain credit by making an additional payment equal to or greater than what you would have paid on a qualifying plan, or by showing you would have qualified for a $0 income-driven payment (§ 685.219(g)(6)). Periods under the Repayment Assistance Plan are excluded. Operationally, the Department has limited buyback to borrowers who already have 120 months of qualifying employment and for whom the purchase would produce forgiveness (MOHELA, https://mohela.studentaid.gov/DL/resourceCenter/PSLFInformation.aspx, accessed 2026-09-04).

**What changed, and what is in flux.** Effective July 1, 2026, no month is credited after a determination that an employer engaged in activities giving it a "substantial illegal purpose," a new term defined at § 685.219(b)(30) and applied through a process at § 685.219(h)–(k). An employer that loses status can regain it after ten years or through an approved corrective action plan (§ 685.219(j)), and a borrower cannot ask for reconsideration on that ground (§ 685.219(g)(7)). This is new, contested machinery. Treat any statement about a specific employer's future PSLF status as unsettled.

## 2. Non-clinical employers that still qualify

Describe these by employer type, not by job title. The job can be entirely non-clinical; only the employer's tax or government status matters.

**Nonprofit hospital and health systems.** A clinical informatics analyst, a clinical liaison, a quality-improvement specialist or a patient-experience lead employed by a 501(c)(3) health system is in qualifying employment on the same terms as a bedside SLP there. Scale: of 5,121 U.S. community hospitals, 2,984 are nongovernment not-for-profit and 913 are state or local government, against 1,224 investor-owned (AHA, Fast Facts on U.S. Hospitals, 2026 edition, 2024 AHA Annual Survey data, https://www.aha.org/statistics/fast-facts-us-hospitals, accessed 2026-09-04). Roughly three in four community hospitals sit in a category that can qualify. Ownership of a given hospital still has to be checked.

**Universities and colleges.** Public universities are state government entities; private nonprofit universities are 501(c)(3) organizations; Tribal colleges are named explicitly (§ 685.219(b)(27)(i)(D)). Research coordinator, instructional designer, academic program manager, grant administrator and clinic administrator roles inside them are qualifying employment.

**State and local government.** Disability Determination Services is the clearest example: DDS work is performed by a State agency under agreement with the Social Security Administration, with the state providing the organizational structure, facilities and personnel (20 CFR 404.1603(c), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-404.1603, accessed 2026-09-04). A disability examiner job at DDS is a state government job. So are public school district administration, county early-intervention program coordination, state Medicaid agency roles and state department of education positions — public school districts are local government entities under § 685.219(b)(27)(i)(A).

**What usually does not qualify:** health-tech and ed-tech startups, teletherapy companies organized for profit, contract therapy staffing firms, most private practices, consultancies, and self-employment. There is no PSLF path through a for-profit employer, however mission-driven it sounds.

## 3. Income-driven repayment as of 2026

The regulation names five IDR plans: REPAYE (also called SAVE), IBR, PAYE, ICR, and the Repayment Assistance Plan (34 CFR 685.209(a), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-685.209, accessed 2026-09-04).

**How income sets the payment.** Discretionary income is defined against the federal poverty guideline: 225% for REPAYE, 150% for IBR and PAYE, 100% for ICR (§ 685.209(b)(4)). IBR charges 15% of income above 150% of the poverty guideline, or 10% for a "new borrower" (§ 685.209(b)(1)). RAP works differently: a base payment set as a share of adjusted gross income, from $120 a month at AGI of $10,000 or less, rising through 1%, 2%, 3% and so on to 10% of AGI above $100,000 (§ 685.209(b)(2)). A drop in AGI moves you down those tiers.

**Recertification is annual.** The Secretary calculates the payment and fixes a 12-month period at that amount, then restarts the process when three monthly payments remain (§ 685.209(l)(4) and (l)(8)). Miss it and the payment reverts to a standard-plan-equivalent amount (§ 685.209(l)(9)).

**A bridge-period income drop can lower the payment before the anniversary.** If the borrower believes the payment does not reflect current income, they may ask for recalculation and submit alternative documentation "to account for circumstances such as a decrease in income since the borrower last filed a tax return" (§ 685.209(l)(6)). This is the single most useful sentence in the lesson: a reader who quits in March does not have to wait until next year's recertification to have the payment reflect the smaller income. A forbearance is granted while the recalculation happens (§ 685.209(l)(7)).

**Plan availability is changing.** RAP is written into regulation and PSLF's qualifying-plan definition allows ICR only for payments received on or before June 30, 2028 (§ 685.219(b)(28)(iv)), which corroborates a 2028 sunset for ICR. Secondary reporting says RAP becomes the only IDR option for loans first borrowed on or after July 1, 2026, and that PAYE and ICR stop accepting new enrollees on July 1, 2026 and end July 1, 2028 (Brookings, https://www.brookings.edu/articles/income-driven-repayment-for-federal-student-loans-from-icr-to-rap/, accessed 2026-09-04). Tell readers to confirm their own plan's status on studentaid.gov before assuming.

## 4. COBRA, and the alternative

COBRA applies to group health plans at employers with 20 or more employees and lets a worker who loses coverage keep the same plan temporarily (U.S. Department of Labor, https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra, accessed 2026-09-04).

- **Duration:** usually up to 18 months after job loss or a reduction in hours (DOL, A Worker's Guide to Health Benefits Under COBRA, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/workers-guide-health-benefits-cobra, accessed 2026-09-04).
- **Cost:** "Your premium cannot exceed 102 percent of the plan's total cost of providing you with coverage. The total premium includes both the share you used to pay as an active employee and the amount your employer used to contribute, plus two percent" (same DOL guide).
- **Election window:** at least 60 days, running from the later of the date the plan provides the election notice or the date you would otherwise lose coverage (same DOL guide).
- **First payment:** plans must give at least 45 days to make the initial payment, and coverage can be terminated if you miss it (same DOL guide).
- **Retroactive:** coverage may start on the election date or be retroactive to the date coverage was lost, depending on plan rules (same DOL guide). In practice this means you can decline to pay, stay uninsured, and elect retroactively inside the window if something happens — but confirm your plan's rule before relying on it.

**Estimating the number.** KFF's 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey, the most recent edition, puts the average annual premium at $9,325 for single coverage and $26,993 for family coverage, with covered workers contributing an average of $1,440 and $6,850 respectively (KFF, https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/ and the 22 October 2025 release, https://www.kff.org/health-costs/annual-family-premiums-for-employer-coverage-rise-6-in-2025-nearing-27000-with-workers-paying-6850-toward-premiums-out-of-their-paychecks/, accessed 2026-09-04). Arithmetic from those averages, not from KFF: single coverage at 102% is about $793 a month against roughly $120 a month withheld while employed, a swing near $675; family coverage at 102% is about $2,294 a month against roughly $571, a swing near $1,725. Use the reader's own benefits statement rather than these averages when possible.

**The marketplace alternative.** Losing job-based coverage triggers a Special Enrollment Period, and quitting counts: "If you leave your job for any reason (even if you quit or get fired) and lose your job-based health insurance, you can enroll in a Marketplace plan," with coverage starting the first day of the month after the loss (HealthCare.gov, https://www.healthcare.gov/have-job-based-coverage/if-you-lose-job-based-coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04). You have 60 days after the loss and can also enroll up to 60 days before it. Savings "are based on your household size and your estimated income for the year you need coverage, not by your employment status" (HealthCare.gov, https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04) — which is why a bridge year with low income often makes a marketplace plan cheaper than COBRA. Two traps: voluntarily dropping COBRA mid-term is not a qualifying event, though COBRA running out is (HealthCare.gov, https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04); and electing COBRA first does not forfeit the marketplace SEP as long as you enroll within 60 days of the original coverage loss.

## 5. ASHA CCC and state licensure

**Dues.** Annual renewal is $250 for certified members and $221 for certified non-members, payable by December 31 each year (ASHA, https://www.asha.org/certification/certification-standards-for-slp-dues-and-fees/, accessed 2026-09-04; the page says "currently" and does not name a year).

**Maintenance.** At least 30 professional development hours in each 3-year certification maintenance interval, including 1 PDH in ethics and 2 PDHs in content that strengthens service to diverse individuals, families and communities, plus a one-time 2 hours in supervision or clinical instruction after the CCC is awarded (effective January 1, 2020). The 30-hour standard as described took effect January 1, 2026 (ASHA, https://www.asha.org/certification/prof-dev-for-2020-certification-standards/ and https://www.asha.org/certification/maintain-ccc/, accessed 2026-09-04). A compliance form is due no later than December 31 of the final year of the interval.

**Lapse and reinstatement — the forum claim, checked.** ASHA's reinstatement pathway states that reinstatement "is the issuing of a new certification, not the continuation of a previous certification," and sets requirements by length of lapse: under 12 months, 10 PDHs and no Praxis; 12 months to 2 years, 20 PDHs plus a passing Praxis score no more than 5 years old; 2 years or more, 30 PDHs plus a passing Praxis score no more than 5 years old. The fee is $400 with ASHA membership or $371 without (ASHA, https://www.asha.org/certification/slp-pathway-to-reinstatement/, accessed 2026-09-04). So the widely repeated claim that a lapse "beyond a few years" forces a Praxis retake is directionally right but wrong on the threshold: the trigger is 12 months, not years, and it is not automatically a retake — an existing passing score under 5 years old satisfies it.

**State licences can usually go inactive, and the rules are state-specific.** New York: "If you are not going to practice your profession in New York State, your registration will remain inactive until you meet the continuing competency requirement (including the lapse in practice provision) and submit a registration renewal application with the appropriate fee," and licensees must be registered in order to practice in the state (NYSED Office of the Professions, https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/speech-language-pathology/continuing-education-speech-language-pathology/questions-and-answers, accessed 2026-09-04). Ohio: Rule 4753-3-11 of the Ohio Administrative Code, "Inactive status of license; restoration," lets a licensee whose licence is inactive request return to active status at any time by application, fee, and proof of at least 20 contact hours of continuing education; the statute behind it is ORC 4753.091, "Classification of license as inactive" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/ohio/Ohio-Admin-Code-4753-3-11 and https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-4753-3-11, accessed 2026-09-04).

**Which non-clinical roles actually need an active licence.** Anything that constitutes practising speech-language pathology in a state — including teletherapy across state lines and supervising clinical fellows — requires that state's licence. Roles that do not involve treating patients (instructional design, UX research, content, project management, customer success, data analysis) generally do not require a licence, but some employers list the CCC as a hiring requirement because the credential is what makes you credible to their clinical customers. I could not find an authoritative source enumerating which non-clinical titles require licensure; this belongs in the hedge list.

## 6. The bridge-budget formula

**Months of runway = liquid savings ÷ monthly gap.**

Monthly gap = (net monthly income you give up − net monthly income you will still have) + (health coverage premium you now pay yourself) + (change in monthly student loan payment) + (other benefits you now buy yourself).

Categories to estimate, in the order to fill them in:

1. **Liquid savings** — cash and taxable investments only; do not count retirement accounts.
2. **One-time inflows** — final paycheque, PTO payout if your state or employer pays it, severance, an HSA balance.
3. **Net monthly income given up** — take-home, not gross, including PRN and side income you are keeping.
4. **Net monthly income you keep** — PRN shifts, contract work, a partner's contribution to shared costs.
5. **Health coverage** — COBRA at 102% of the full premium, or the marketplace net premium after subsidies estimated on your bridge-year income. Add dental and vision separately.
6. **Insurance the employer was paying for** — short- and long-term disability, group life. Replacing these costs real money.
7. **Change in loan payment** — could go down if you request an IDR recalculation on lower income (see § 685.209(l)(6)), or up if a $0 IDR payment ends. Net the change; do not assume it is a cost.
8. **Credential upkeep** — ASHA $250 or $221, state licence renewal, CE hours if you intend to keep the CCC current rather than let it lapse.
9. **Taxes** — if bridge income is 1099, add roughly self-employment tax and quarterly estimates; if income drops a lot, withholding falls too.
10. **Job-search costs** — a course, a certificate, a conference, resume help.
11. **Retirement match forgone** — not cash flow, but write it down so the decision is made with eyes open.

Then: runway in months = line 1 plus line 2, divided by the monthly gap. Add a floor: subtract three months of expenses you refuse to touch before dividing.

## What the lesson can state with confidence

1. PSLF requires 120 qualifying monthly payments made after October 1, 2007, and they do not have to be consecutive.
2. A qualifying employer is a government entity, a 501(c)(3), a Tribal college, or a nonprofit providing a defined public service; for-profit employers and for-profit private practices never qualify.
3. Full time means an average of at least 30 hours a week, and you may add up to that across more than one qualifying job.
4. Leaving for a non-qualifying employer pauses the count and does not erase earned credit, but you must be back in qualifying full-time employment at the 120th payment and when you apply.
5. Plenty of non-clinical jobs are inside qualifying employers: nonprofit health systems, universities, state agencies such as Disability Determination Services, and school districts.
6. Federal regulation lets a borrower ask for an income-driven payment to be recalculated mid-year using documentation of a drop in income since their last tax return.
7. COBRA usually lasts up to 18 months, may be priced at up to 102% of the plan's full cost, must be offered with at least a 60-day election window, and allows at least 45 days for the first payment.
8. Losing job-based coverage because you quit still qualifies you for a marketplace Special Enrollment Period, and marketplace savings depend on estimated income for the year, not employment status.
9. Average 2025 employer premiums were $9,325 a year for single and $26,993 for family coverage, of which the average worker paid $1,440 and $6,850 — so the employer's share is the number that lands on you.
10. ASHA reinstatement requires a Praxis score no more than five years old once certification has been expired more than 12 months, along with 20 or 30 PDHs depending on the length of lapse and a $400 or $371 fee.

5. Two things I could not source authoritatively: which specific non-clinical job titles legally require an active SLP licence (it depends on state practice acts, not job titles), and whether a given health plan makes COBRA retroactive to the coverage-loss date — plan rules vary and the DOL guide says so explicitly.
