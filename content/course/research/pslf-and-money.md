# The loan is a calendar: PSLF, the pay-cut budget, COBRA — research notes

Compiled 2026-09-04 for the Transition OS money lesson. Audience: U.S. SLPs deciding whether and when to leave clinical work. Every claim carries a URL and access date; unsettled points are flagged and repeated in the hedge list. Not legal, tax or financial advice.

## 1. PSLF mechanics

The program lives at 34 CFR 685.219; citations here are to the current eCFR text (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-685.219, accessed 2026-09-04), source note [91 FR 23897, May 1, 2026].

**Qualifying employers** (§ 685.219(b)(27)): a U.S.-based federal, state, local or Tribal government organization; a public child or family service agency; a 501(c)(3); a Tribal college or university; and a nonprofit providing a defined "non-governmental public service" that is not organized for profit, not a labor union and not a partisan political organization. For-profit companies and for-profit private practices do not qualify. "Employee" means someone W-2'd by the employer or by its contracted payroll service (§ 685.219(b)(9)); a 1099 contractor to a nonprofit is not its employee.

**Full time is 30 hours, and jobs add up.** Full-time means qualifying employment "in one or more jobs" averaging at least 30 hours a week over the period certified (§ 685.219(b)(11)(i)(A)). Two 15-hour nonprofit jobs qualify; 25 nonprofit hours plus 15 for-profit hours do not, because only qualifying employment counts toward the 30.

**120 payments, and employment on both ends.** You need the equivalent of 120 monthly qualifying payments made after October 1, 2007, on Direct Loans, and must be employed full-time by a qualifying employer both when the 120th is satisfied and when you apply (§ 685.219(c)(1)). A month counts only if you were so employed "at any point during the month" (§ 685.219(c)(2)(vi)). Payments need not be consecutive. Qualifying plans: the IDR plans, the 10-year standard plan, any plan paying at least that amount, RAP, and ICR only for payments received on or before June 30, 2028 (§ 685.219(b)(28)).

**Certifying employment.** Federal Student Aid's guidance is to certify annually and again on any change of employer or status (https://studentaid.gov/articles/manage-your-pslf-progress/, https://studentaid.gov/pslf/, accessed 2026-09-04 — see hedge list). The regulation only requires certification at application (§ 685.219(e)(3)), so annual filing is best practice, not a deadline.

**Leaving for a non-qualifying employer.** Nothing in § 685.219 removes credit already earned; months elsewhere are not credited, so the count pauses rather than resets. The catch is § 685.219(c)(1)(ii): you must be back in qualifying full-time employment at the 120th payment and at application. A three-year detour into a for-profit costs three years, not the eight already banked.

**Buyback exists in regulation.** For months when you worked full-time for a qualifying employer but sat in a deferment or forbearance not on the credited list, you may obtain credit by paying at least what a qualifying plan would have charged, or by showing you would have owed $0 on IDR (§ 685.219(g)(6)). RAP periods are excluded. Operationally the Department limits buyback to borrowers who already have 120 months of qualifying employment and for whom it produces forgiveness (MOHELA, https://mohela.studentaid.gov/DL/resourceCenter/PSLFInformation.aspx, accessed 2026-09-04).

**In flux.** Effective July 1, 2026, no month is credited after a determination that an employer has a "substantial illegal purpose" (§ 685.219(b)(30), applied through (h)–(k)). A disqualified employer regains status after ten years or an approved corrective action plan (§ 685.219(j)); borrowers cannot seek reconsideration on that ground (§ 685.219(g)(7)). This machinery is new and contested, so any claim about a specific employer's future status is unsettled.

## 2. Non-clinical employers that still qualify

Describe these by employer type, not job title. The work can be wholly non-clinical; only the employer's status matters.

**Nonprofit hospital and health systems.** A clinical informatics analyst, clinical liaison, quality-improvement specialist or patient-experience lead at a 501(c)(3) health system qualifies on the same terms as a bedside SLP there. Of 5,121 U.S. community hospitals, 2,984 are nongovernment not-for-profit and 913 state or local government, against 1,224 investor-owned (AHA, Fast Facts on U.S. Hospitals, 2026 edition, 2024 survey data, https://www.aha.org/statistics/fast-facts-us-hospitals, accessed 2026-09-04). Check the individual hospital's ownership.

**Universities and colleges.** Public universities are state entities, private nonprofits are 501(c)(3)s, Tribal colleges are named explicitly (§ 685.219(b)(27)(i)(D)). Research coordinator, instructional designer, program manager and grant administrator roles there all qualify.

**State and local government.** Disability Determination Services is the cleanest example: DDS work is performed by a State agency under agreement with SSA, the state providing structure, facilities and personnel (20 CFR 404.1603(c), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-404.1603, accessed 2026-09-04). School district administration, county early-intervention coordination, state Medicaid and state education roles are government employers too, under § 685.219(b)(27)(i)(A).

**Usually not qualifying:** health-tech and ed-tech startups, for-profit teletherapy companies, contract therapy staffing firms, most private practices, consultancies, self-employment. There is no PSLF path through a for-profit employer, however mission-driven its branding.

## 3. Income-driven repayment as of 2026

Five IDR plans exist in regulation: REPAYE (also called SAVE), IBR, PAYE, ICR and the Repayment Assistance Plan (34 CFR 685.209(a), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-685.209, accessed 2026-09-04).

**How income sets the payment.** Discretionary income runs against the federal poverty guideline at 225% for REPAYE, 150% for IBR and PAYE, 100% for ICR (§ 685.209(b)(4)). IBR charges 15% of income above 150% of the guideline, or 10% for a "new borrower" (§ 685.209(b)(1)). RAP uses a base payment tied to AGI: $120 a month at $10,000 or less, then 1%, 2%, 3% upward by $10,000 bands to 10% above $100,000 (§ 685.209(b)(2)). A drop in AGI moves you down those tiers.

**Recertification is annual.** The payment is fixed for 12 months and the process restarts when three payments remain (§ 685.209(l)(4), (l)(8)). Miss it and the payment reverts to a standard-plan-equivalent amount (§ 685.209(l)(9)).

**A bridge-period income drop can lower the payment before the anniversary.** A borrower may request recalculation with alternative documentation "to account for circumstances such as a decrease in income since the borrower last filed a tax return" (§ 685.209(l)(6)); forbearance covers the processing time (§ 685.209(l)(7)). Quit in March and you need not wait for next year's recertification.

**Availability is changing.** RAP is in regulation, and PSLF credit for ICR payments ends after June 30, 2028 (§ 685.219(b)(28)(iv)). Secondary reporting says RAP becomes the only IDR option for loans first borrowed on or after July 1, 2026, and PAYE and ICR close to new enrollees July 1, 2026 and end July 1, 2028 (Brookings, https://www.brookings.edu/articles/income-driven-repayment-for-federal-student-loans-from-icr-to-rap/, accessed 2026-09-04). Confirm your own plan on studentaid.gov.

## 4. COBRA, and the alternative

COBRA applies to group health plans at employers with 20 or more employees (DOL, https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra, accessed 2026-09-04). The rest of this list is from DOL's *A Worker's Guide to Health Benefits Under COBRA* (https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/workers-guide-health-benefits-cobra, accessed 2026-09-04):

- **Duration:** usually up to 18 months after job loss or reduced hours.
- **Cost:** "Your premium cannot exceed 102 percent of the plan's total cost of providing you with coverage," a total that "includes both the share you used to pay as an active employee and the amount your employer used to contribute, plus two percent."
- **Election:** at least 60 days, from the later of the election-notice date or the date coverage would otherwise be lost.
- **First payment:** at least 45 days; miss it and the plan can terminate coverage.
- **Retroactive:** coverage "may start from the date you elect it or be retroactive to the date coverage was lost depending on the plan rules." Confirm your plan's rule first.

**Estimating a monthly number.** KFF's 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey, the most recent, puts average annual premiums at $9,325 single and $26,993 family, with covered workers contributing $1,440 and $6,850 (https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/, 22 October 2025 release at https://www.kff.org/health-costs/annual-family-premiums-for-employer-coverage-rise-6-in-2025-nearing-27000-with-workers-paying-6850-toward-premiums-out-of-their-paychecks/, accessed 2026-09-04). Arithmetic from those averages, not from KFF: single coverage at 102% is about $793 a month against roughly $120 withheld while employed, a swing near $675; family about $2,294 against $571, a swing near $1,725. Prefer the reader's own benefits statement.

**The marketplace alternative.** Losing job-based coverage triggers a Special Enrollment Period, and quitting counts: "If you leave your job for any reason (even if you quit or get fired) and lose your job-based health insurance, you can enroll in a Marketplace plan," with coverage starting the first day of the month after the loss (https://www.healthcare.gov/have-job-based-coverage/if-you-lose-job-based-coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04). You get 60 days after the loss, and may enroll up to 60 days before. Savings "are based on your household size and your estimated income for the year you need coverage, not by your employment status" (https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04), which is why a low-income bridge year often beats COBRA on price. Two traps: dropping COBRA mid-term voluntarily is not a qualifying event, though COBRA running out is (https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04); and electing COBRA does not forfeit the SEP if you enroll within 60 days of the original loss.

## 5. ASHA CCC and state licensure

**Dues:** $250 a year for certified members, $221 for certified non-members, due December 31 (https://www.asha.org/certification/certification-standards-for-slp-dues-and-fees/, accessed 2026-09-04; the page says "currently", naming no year).

**Maintenance:** at least 30 professional development hours per 3-year interval, including 1 PDH in ethics and 2 PDHs in content strengthening service to diverse individuals, families and communities, plus a one-time 2 hours in supervision after the CCC is awarded (effective January 1, 2020). The standard as described took effect January 1, 2026 (https://www.asha.org/certification/prof-dev-for-2020-certification-standards/, https://www.asha.org/certification/maintain-ccc/, accessed 2026-09-04). A compliance form is due by December 31 of the final year.

**Lapse and reinstatement — the forum claim, checked.** ASHA states reinstatement "is the issuing of a new certification, not the continuation of a previous certification," and sets requirements by lapse: under 12 months, 10 PDHs and no Praxis; 12 months to 2 years, 20 PDHs plus a passing Praxis score no more than 5 years old; 2 years or more, 30 PDHs plus the same Praxis condition. Fee $400 with ASHA membership, $371 without (https://www.asha.org/certification/slp-pathway-to-reinstatement/, accessed 2026-09-04). The common claim that lapsing "beyond a few years" forces a Praxis retake is directionally right but wrong on the threshold: the trigger is 12 months, not years, and a passing score under 5 years old satisfies it without retaking.

**State licences can usually go inactive; rules are state-specific.** New York: "If you are not going to practice your profession in New York State, your registration will remain inactive until you meet the continuing competency requirement... and submit a registration renewal application with the appropriate fee"; you must be registered to practise there (https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/speech-language-pathology/continuing-education-speech-language-pathology/questions-and-answers, accessed 2026-09-04). Ohio: Rule 4753-3-11, "Inactive status of license; restoration," lets an inactive licensee return to active at any time by application, fee, and proof of at least 20 contact hours of continuing education (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/ohio/Ohio-Admin-Code-4753-3-11, https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-4753-3-11, accessed 2026-09-04).

**Which non-clinical roles need an active licence.** A licence is needed for anything constituting the practice of speech-language pathology in a state, including cross-state teletherapy and supervising clinical fellows. Roles that never treat patients — instructional design, UX research, content, project management, customer success, data analysis — generally do not, though employers often list the CCC because it makes you credible to clinical customers. No authoritative source enumerates which titles require it.

## 6. The bridge-budget formula

**Months of runway = liquid savings ÷ monthly gap**, where monthly gap = (net income given up − net income you keep) + coverage premium you now pay yourself + change in monthly loan payment + benefits you now buy yourself.

Categories to estimate:

1. **Liquid savings** — cash and taxable investments, not retirement accounts.
2. **One-time inflows** — final paycheque, PTO payout if paid, severance, HSA.
3. **Net monthly income given up** — take-home, not gross.
4. **Net monthly income you keep** — PRN shifts, contract work, a partner's share.
5. **Health coverage** — COBRA at 102% of the full premium, or the marketplace net premium after subsidies on bridge-year income. Dental and vision are separate.
6. **Insurance the employer paid for** — short- and long-term disability, group life.
7. **Change in loan payment** — down if you request an IDR recalculation (§ 685.209(l)(6)), up if a $0 payment ends. Net it; do not assume it is a cost.
8. **Credential upkeep** — ASHA $250 or $221, licence renewal, CE hours if you stay current.
9. **Taxes** — 1099 bridge income adds self-employment tax and quarterly estimates; a big income drop cuts withholding.
10. **Job-search costs** — a course, certificate or conference.
11. **Retirement match forgone** — not cash flow, but note it.

Runway = (1 + 2) ÷ monthly gap. Add a floor: subtract three months of untouchable expenses first.

## What the lesson can state with confidence

1. PSLF requires 120 qualifying monthly payments made after October 1, 2007, and they need not be consecutive.
2. A qualifying employer is a government entity, a 501(c)(3), a Tribal college, or a nonprofit providing a defined public service; for-profit employers and private practices never qualify.
3. Full time means an average of at least 30 hours a week, reachable across more than one qualifying job.
4. Leaving for a non-qualifying employer pauses the count without erasing earned credit, but you must be back in qualifying employment at the 120th payment and at application.
5. Many non-clinical jobs sit inside qualifying employers: nonprofit health systems, universities, state agencies such as Disability Determination Services, school districts.
6. Federal regulation lets a borrower have an income-driven payment recalculated mid-year on documentation of an income drop since their last tax return.
7. COBRA usually lasts up to 18 months, may cost up to 102% of the plan's full price, and carries at least a 60-day election window and 45 days for the first payment.
8. Quitting still counts as losing job-based coverage for a marketplace Special Enrollment Period, whose savings depend on estimated income, not employment status.
9. Average 2025 employer premiums were $9,325 a year single and $26,993 family, of which workers paid $1,440 and $6,850; the employer's share is what lands on you.
10. ASHA reinstatement requires a Praxis score no more than five years old once certification has lapsed more than 12 months, plus 20 or 30 PDHs and a $400 or $371 fee.

## What the lesson must hedge or tell the reader to verify themselves

1. Studentaid.gov renders client-side, so I could not quote Federal Student Aid on filing frequency; annual certification plus certification on every employer change is the widely reported guidance, and readers should confirm it in the PSLF Help Tool.
2. PSLF's "substantial illegal purpose" employer-disqualification process takes effect July 1, 2026 and is untested, so promise nothing about a specific employer qualifying five years out.
3. IDR is mid-transition: RAP is in regulation and ICR credit ends after June 30, 2028, but the PAYE and ICR enrollment cut-offs come from secondary reporting, so readers must check their own plan.
4. I verified no individual employer's nonprofit or government status; readers must look theirs up in the PSLF employer search, because a nonprofit-sounding name is not evidence.
5. Two things I could not source authoritatively: which non-clinical job titles legally require an active SLP licence (it turns on state practice acts, not titles), and whether a given plan makes COBRA retroactive to the coverage-loss date, which DOL says varies by plan.
