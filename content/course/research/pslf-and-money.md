# The loan is a calendar: PSLF, the pay-cut budget, COBRA — research notes

Compiled 2026-09-04 for the Transition OS money lesson. Audience: U.S. SLPs deciding whether and when to leave clinical employment. Every claim carries a URL and access date. Where a rule is unsettled or I could not verify it from a primary source, it is flagged and repeated in the hedge list. Not legal, tax or financial advice.

## 1. PSLF mechanics

The program lives at 34 CFR 685.219. All citations in this section are to the current eCFR text (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-685.219, accessed 2026-09-04), source note [91 FR 23897, May 1, 2026].

**Qualifying employers** (§ 685.219(b)(27)): a U.S.-based federal, state, local or Tribal government organization; a public child or family service agency; a 501(c)(3) organization; a Tribal college or university; and a nonprofit providing a defined "non-governmental public service" that is not organized for profit, not a labor union and not a partisan political organization. For-profit companies do not qualify, and neither do for-profit private practices. "Employee" means someone issued a W-2 by the employer, or W-2'd through a payroll service contracted to it, or a contracted employee in a role state law bars the employer from filling directly (§ 685.219(b)(9)). A 1099 contractor to a nonprofit is not its employee.

**Full time is 30 hours, and jobs add up.** Full-time means working in qualifying employment "in one or more jobs" a minimum average of 30 hours per week during the period certified (§ 685.219(b)(11)(i)(A)). Two 15-hour nonprofit jobs qualify; 25 hours at a nonprofit plus 15 at a for-profit does not, because only qualifying employment counts toward the 30.

**120 payments, and employment on both ends.** You need the equivalent of 120 monthly qualifying payments made after October 1, 2007, on Direct Loans, and you must be employed full-time by a qualifying employer both when the 120th payment is satisfied and when you apply (§ 685.219(c)(1)). A month counts only if you were employed full-time by a qualifying employer "at any point during the month" (§ 685.219(c)(2)(vi)). Payments need not be consecutive. Qualifying plans are the IDR plans, the 10-year standard plan, any plan paying at least the 10-year standard amount, RAP, and ICR only for payments received on or before June 30, 2028 (§ 685.219(b)(28)).

**Certifying employment.** Federal Student Aid's guidance is to certify annually and again on any change of employer or employment status (https://studentaid.gov/articles/manage-your-pslf-progress/, https://studentaid.gov/pslf/, accessed 2026-09-04 — see hedge list; studentaid.gov renders client-side and I could not pull the sentence verbatim). The regulation requires certification at application (§ 685.219(e)(3)), so annual filing is best practice, not a legal deadline.

**Leaving for a non-qualifying employer.** Nothing in § 685.219 removes credit already earned; months at a non-qualifying employer simply are not credited, so the count pauses rather than resets. The catch is § 685.219(c)(1)(ii): you must be back in qualifying full-time employment at the 120th payment and at application. A three-year detour into a for-profit costs three years, not the eight you already had.

**Buyback exists in regulation.** For months when you were employed full-time by a qualifying employer but sat in a deferment or forbearance not on the credited list, you may obtain credit by paying at least what you would have paid on a qualifying plan, or by showing you would have owed $0 on an IDR plan (§ 685.219(g)(6)). Repayment Assistance Plan periods are excluded. Operationally the Department limits buyback to borrowers who already have 120 months of qualifying employment and for whom the purchase produces forgiveness (MOHELA, https://mohela.studentaid.gov/DL/resourceCenter/PSLFInformation.aspx, accessed 2026-09-04).

**In flux.** Effective July 1, 2026, no month is credited after a determination that an employer has a "substantial illegal purpose," a new term defined at § 685.219(b)(30) and applied through § 685.219(h)–(k). A disqualified employer can regain status after ten years or via an approved corrective action plan (§ 685.219(j)), and borrowers cannot seek reconsideration on that ground (§ 685.219(g)(7)). This machinery is new and contested. Any claim about a specific employer's future PSLF status is unsettled.

## 2. Non-clinical employers that still qualify

Describe these by employer type, not job title. The work can be entirely non-clinical; only the employer's tax or government status matters.

**Nonprofit hospital and health systems.** A clinical informatics analyst, clinical liaison, quality-improvement specialist or patient-experience lead employed by a 501(c)(3) health system is in qualifying employment on the same terms as a bedside SLP there. Scale: of 5,121 U.S. community hospitals, 2,984 are nongovernment not-for-profit and 913 are state or local government, against 1,224 investor-owned (AHA, Fast Facts on U.S. Hospitals, 2026 edition, 2024 AHA Annual Survey data, https://www.aha.org/statistics/fast-facts-us-hospitals, accessed 2026-09-04). Roughly three in four community hospitals sit in a category that can qualify; the individual hospital still has to be checked.

**Universities and colleges.** Public universities are state entities, private nonprofit universities are 501(c)(3)s, and Tribal colleges are named explicitly (§ 685.219(b)(27)(i)(D)). Research coordinator, instructional designer, program manager, grant administrator and clinic administrator roles inside them qualify.

**State and local government.** Disability Determination Services is the cleanest example: DDS work is performed by a State agency under agreement with SSA, with the state providing the organizational structure, facilities and personnel (20 CFR 404.1603(c), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-404.1603, accessed 2026-09-04). Public school district administration, county early-intervention coordination, state Medicaid and state department of education roles are also local or state government under § 685.219(b)(27)(i)(A).

**Usually not qualifying:** health-tech and ed-tech startups, for-profit teletherapy companies, contract therapy staffing firms, most private practices, consultancies, and self-employment. There is no PSLF path through a for-profit employer, however mission-driven the branding.

## 3. Income-driven repayment as of 2026

Five IDR plans exist in regulation: REPAYE (also called SAVE), IBR, PAYE, ICR, and the Repayment Assistance Plan (34 CFR 685.209(a), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-685.209, accessed 2026-09-04).

**How income sets the payment.** Discretionary income runs against the federal poverty guideline at 225% for REPAYE, 150% for IBR and PAYE, and 100% for ICR (§ 685.209(b)(4)). IBR charges 15% of income above 150% of the guideline, or 10% for a "new borrower" (§ 685.209(b)(1)). RAP instead uses a base payment tied to adjusted gross income: $120 a month at AGI of $10,000 or less, then 1%, 2%, 3% and upward by $10,000 bands to 10% of AGI above $100,000 (§ 685.209(b)(2)). A drop in AGI moves you down those tiers.

**Recertification is annual.** The payment is fixed for a 12-month period, and the process restarts when three payments remain (§ 685.209(l)(4), (l)(8)). Miss it and the payment reverts to a standard-plan-equivalent amount (§ 685.209(l)(9)).

**A bridge-period income drop can lower the payment before the anniversary.** A borrower may request recalculation and submit alternative documentation "to account for circumstances such as a decrease in income since the borrower last filed a tax return" (§ 685.209(l)(6)); forbearance is granted while it is processed (§ 685.209(l)(7)). This is the most useful sentence in the lesson: quit in March and you do not wait until next year's recertification.

**Availability is changing.** RAP is in regulation, and PSLF credit for ICR payments ends after June 30, 2028 (§ 685.219(b)(28)(iv)). Secondary reporting says RAP becomes the only IDR option for loans first borrowed on or after July 1, 2026, and that PAYE and ICR close to new enrollees on July 1, 2026 and end July 1, 2028 (Brookings, https://www.brookings.edu/articles/income-driven-repayment-for-federal-student-loans-from-icr-to-rap/, accessed 2026-09-04). Readers should confirm their own plan on studentaid.gov.

## 4. COBRA, and the alternative

COBRA applies to group health plans at employers with 20 or more employees (DOL, https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra, accessed 2026-09-04). The following are from DOL's *A Worker's Guide to Health Benefits Under COBRA* (https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/workers-guide-health-benefits-cobra, accessed 2026-09-04):

- **Duration:** usually up to 18 months after job loss or reduced hours.
- **Cost:** "Your premium cannot exceed 102 percent of the plan's total cost of providing you with coverage. The total premium includes both the share you used to pay as an active employee and the amount your employer used to contribute, plus two percent."
- **Election:** at least 60 days, running from the later of the election-notice date or the date coverage would otherwise be lost.
- **First payment:** plans must allow at least 45 days, and can terminate coverage if you miss it.
- **Retroactive:** coverage "may start from the date you elect it or be retroactive to the date coverage was lost depending on the plan rules." Confirm your plan's rule before relying on a retroactive election.

**Estimating a monthly number.** KFF's 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey, the most recent edition, puts average annual premiums at $9,325 single and $26,993 family, with covered workers contributing $1,440 and $6,850 (https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/ and the 22 October 2025 release, https://www.kff.org/health-costs/annual-family-premiums-for-employer-coverage-rise-6-in-2025-nearing-27000-with-workers-paying-6850-toward-premiums-out-of-their-paychecks/, accessed 2026-09-04). Arithmetic from those averages, not from KFF: single coverage at 102% is about $793 a month against roughly $120 withheld while employed, a swing near $675; family coverage at 102% is about $2,294 against roughly $571, a swing near $1,725. Use the reader's own benefits statement where possible.

**The marketplace alternative.** Losing job-based coverage triggers a Special Enrollment Period, and quitting counts: "If you leave your job for any reason (even if you quit or get fired) and lose your job-based health insurance, you can enroll in a Marketplace plan," with coverage starting the first day of the month after the loss (https://www.healthcare.gov/have-job-based-coverage/if-you-lose-job-based-coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04). You get 60 days after the loss and can also enroll up to 60 days before. Savings "are based on your household size and your estimated income for the year you need coverage, not by your employment status" (https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04) — which is why a low-income bridge year often makes a marketplace plan cheaper than COBRA. Two traps: voluntarily dropping COBRA mid-term is not a qualifying event, though COBRA running out is (https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/, accessed 2026-09-04); and electing COBRA does not forfeit the SEP as long as you enroll within 60 days of the original coverage loss.

## 5. ASHA CCC and state licensure

**Dues:** $250 a year for certified members, $221 for certified non-members, payable by December 31 (https://www.asha.org/certification/certification-standards-for-slp-dues-and-fees/, accessed 2026-09-04; the page says "currently" and names no year).

**Maintenance:** at least 30 professional development hours per 3-year certification maintenance interval, including 1 PDH in ethics and 2 PDHs in content strengthening service to diverse individuals, families and communities, plus a one-time 2 hours in supervision or clinical instruction after the CCC is awarded (effective January 1, 2020). The standard as described took effect January 1, 2026 (https://www.asha.org/certification/prof-dev-for-2020-certification-standards/, https://www.asha.org/certification/maintain-ccc/, accessed 2026-09-04). A compliance form is due by December 31 of the interval's final year.

**Lapse and reinstatement — the forum claim, checked.** ASHA states reinstatement "is the issuing of a new certification, not the continuation of a previous certification," and sets requirements by length of lapse: under 12 months, 10 PDHs and no Praxis; 12 months to 2 years, 20 PDHs plus a passing Praxis score no more than 5 years old; 2 years or more, 30 PDHs plus a passing Praxis score no more than 5 years old. Fee $400 with ASHA membership, $371 without (https://www.asha.org/certification/slp-pathway-to-reinstatement/, accessed 2026-09-04). So the common claim that lapsing "beyond a few years" forces a Praxis retake is directionally right but wrong on the threshold: the trigger is 12 months, not years, and an existing passing score under 5 years old satisfies it without retaking.

**State licences can usually go inactive; rules are state-specific.** New York: "If you are not going to practice your profession in New York State, your registration will remain inactive until you meet the continuing competency requirement (including the lapse in practice provision) and submit a registration renewal application with the appropriate fee," and you must be registered to practise in the state (https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/speech-language-pathology/continuing-education-speech-language-pathology/questions-and-answers, accessed 2026-09-04). Ohio: Rule 4753-3-11 of the Ohio Administrative Code, "Inactive status of license; restoration," lets a licensee on inactive status request return to active at any time by application, fee, and proof of at least 20 contact hours of continuing education; the enabling statute is ORC 4753.091, "Classification of license as inactive" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/ohio/Ohio-Admin-Code-4753-3-11, https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-4753-3-11, accessed 2026-09-04).

**Which non-clinical roles need an active licence.** Anything that constitutes practising speech-language pathology in a state, including cross-state teletherapy and supervising clinical fellows, needs that state's licence. Roles that do not treat patients — instructional design, UX research, content, project management, customer success, data analysis — generally do not, though some employers list the CCC as a hiring requirement because it makes you credible to their clinical customers. I found no authoritative source enumerating which non-clinical titles require licensure; see the hedge list.

## 6. The bridge-budget formula

**Months of runway = liquid savings ÷ monthly gap**, where

monthly gap = (net monthly income given up − net monthly income you keep) + coverage premium you now pay yourself + change in monthly loan payment + benefits you now buy yourself.

Categories to estimate, in filling order:

1. **Liquid savings** — cash and taxable investments only, not retirement accounts.
2. **One-time inflows** — final paycheque, PTO payout if paid in your state or by your employer, severance, HSA balance.
3. **Net monthly income given up** — take-home, not gross.
4. **Net monthly income you keep** — PRN shifts, contract work, a partner's share of shared costs.
5. **Health coverage** — COBRA at 102% of the full premium, or the marketplace net premium after subsidies estimated on bridge-year income. Dental and vision are separate.
6. **Insurance the employer paid for** — short- and long-term disability, group life.
7. **Change in loan payment** — down if you request an IDR recalculation on lower income (§ 685.209(l)(6)), up if a $0 IDR payment ends. Net it; do not assume it is a cost.
8. **Credential upkeep** — ASHA $250 or $221, state licence renewal, CE hours if you intend to stay current rather than lapse.
9. **Taxes** — 1099 bridge income adds self-employment tax and quarterly estimates; a big income drop also cuts withholding.
10. **Job-search costs** — a course, a certificate, a conference.
11. **Retirement match forgone** — not cash flow, but write it down.

Runway = (1 + 2) ÷ monthly gap. Add a floor: subtract three months of expenses you refuse to touch before dividing.

## What the lesson can state with confidence

1. PSLF requires 120 qualifying monthly payments made after October 1, 2007, and they need not be consecutive.
2. A qualifying employer is a government entity, a 501(c)(3), a Tribal college, or a nonprofit providing a defined public service; for-profit employers and for-profit private practices never qualify.
3. Full time means an average of at least 30 hours a week, and you may reach it across more than one qualifying job.
4. Leaving for a non-qualifying employer pauses the count and does not erase earned credit, but you must be back in qualifying full-time employment at the 120th payment and at application.
5. Many non-clinical jobs sit inside qualifying employers: nonprofit health systems, universities, state agencies such as Disability Determination Services, and school districts.
6. Federal regulation lets a borrower ask for an income-driven payment to be recalculated mid-year on documentation of an income drop since their last tax return.
7. COBRA usually lasts up to 18 months, may be priced at up to 102% of the plan's full cost, carries at least a 60-day election window, and allows at least 45 days for the first payment.
8. Losing job-based coverage because you quit still qualifies you for a marketplace Special Enrollment Period, and marketplace savings depend on estimated income for the year, not employment status.
9. Average 2025 employer premiums were $9,325 a year single and $26,993 family, of which the average worker paid $1,440 and $6,850 — the employer's share is the part that lands on you.
10. ASHA reinstatement requires a Praxis score no more than five years old once certification has been expired more than 12 months, plus 20 or 30 PDHs depending on the lapse and a $400 or $371 fee.

## What the lesson must hedge or tell the reader to verify themselves

1. I could not pull Federal Student Aid's own sentence on how often to file the PSLF form, because studentaid.gov renders client-side; annual certification plus certification on every employer change is the widely reported guidance, and the reader should confirm it in the PSLF Help Tool.
2. PSLF's new "substantial illegal purpose" employer-disqualification process takes effect July 1, 2026 and is new and untested, so no lesson should promise that a specific employer will still qualify in five years.
3. The IDR landscape is mid-transition: RAP is in regulation and PSLF credit for ICR ends after June 30, 2028, but the exact enrollment cut-offs for PAYE and ICR come from secondary reporting, so each reader must check their own plan on studentaid.gov.
4. I verified no individual employer's nonprofit or government status; readers must look their own employer up in the PSLF employer search, because a nonprofit-sounding name is not evidence.
5. Two things I could not source authoritatively: which specific non-clinical job titles legally require an active SLP licence (it turns on state practice acts, not titles), and whether a given plan makes COBRA retroactive to the coverage-loss date, which DOL says varies by plan.
