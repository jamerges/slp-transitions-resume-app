# Negotiating the first non-clinical offer: research notes

Compiled 2026-09-04 for the Transition OS negotiation lesson. Audience: U.S. SLPs taking a first offer in customer success, clinical liaison, project management, clinical informatics, instructional design, content, or data. Every figure carries its source, URL, access date, and sample size where the source gives one. Our own path ranges come from content/research-facts.md.

## 1. Does negotiating work, and by how much?

Most people do not ask. In Pew's April 2023 survey of 5,188 employed U.S. workers, only 32% of men and 28% of women said they asked for higher pay the last time they were hired (Pew Research Center, https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/04/05/when-negotiating-starting-salaries-most-us-women-and-men-dont-ask-for-higher-pay/, accessed 2026-09-04). ZipRecruiter's Q4 2025 New Hires Survey (n=1,500) put the share who negotiated at 30% (reported by The Hill via Yahoo Finance, https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/more-quarter-hires-taking-pay-230602222.html, 2026-03-19, accessed 2026-09-04).

Asking usually pays. In the Pew sample, among those who asked, 28% got exactly what they asked for and 38% got more than the original offer but less than they asked; only 35% got nothing extra. So roughly two in three askers improved the offer (Pew, same URL). Fidelity's 2022 survey (Engine Insights, n=1,524 working adults 25 to 70, March 8 to 14, 2022) found 85% of people who countered on salary, benefits, or both got at least some of what they asked for (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2022/05/13/85-percent-of-americans-who-negtiated-a-job-offer-were-successful.html, accessed 2026-09-04); Fidelity's own page cites 87% from its December 2022 Career Assessment Study, no sample given (https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/smart-money/how-to-negotiate-salary, accessed 2026-09-04). ZipRecruiter's Q2 2026 New Hires Survey (n>1,500 people hired in the prior six months) reports 90% of women and 91% of men who negotiated "walk away with better offers" (https://www.ziprecruiter-research.org/economic-insights-research/new-hire-survey-2026-q2, accessed 2026-09-04).

How much: the one peer-reviewed field study on new hires, Marks and Harold (2011, Journal of Organizational Behavior 32:371-394), followed 149 newly hired employees across industries and found those who negotiated raised starting pay by about $5,000 a year on average ($4,913) versus those who accepted; competing and collaborating strategies produced the gains, accommodating did not (abstract https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/job.671; summary https://www.strategy-business.com/article/11115, accessed 2026-09-04). Babcock and Laschever's Carnegie Mellon master's-graduate data: 57% of men and 7% of women negotiated, and negotiators raised starting pay 7.4% on average, about $4,083 (Women Don't Ask, Princeton, 2003; figures as quoted in Silva and Galbraith, College & Research Libraries, 2018, https://crl.acrl.org/index.php/crl/article/view/16689/18646, accessed 2026-09-04). Note the sample is elite business graduates, and later data from Kray, Kennedy and Lee (2024; ~1,000 MBA grads, then ~2,000 alumni) found women now negotiate at least as often as men (PON Harvard, https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/salary-negotiations/in-salary-negotiations-women-do-ask, accessed 2026-09-04).

Rescinded offers are rare. Hart, Bear and Ren (2024, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes; seven studies, >3,000 participants) surveyed hiring managers who reported extending an average of 26.9 offers in their careers and withdrawing 1.73 after a candidate negotiated, and found candidates consistently overestimate the risk (George Mason University, https://www.gmu.edu/news/2024-07/scared-negotiate-job-offers-do-it-anyway-heres-why, accessed 2026-09-04). In a 2013 Salary.com survey of over 1,000 employees and employers, 87% of employers said they had never rescinded an offer after a negotiation and 84% said they expect candidates to negotiate (secondary: https://four-leaf.ai/blog/what-hiring-managers-do-when-you-negotiate, accessed 2026-09-04; original Salary.com page no longer live). XpertHR's 2021 survey of 324 U.S. companies found 89% open to negotiating after an offer (InHerSight, https://www.inhersight.com/blog/interview/can-you-lose-a-job-offer-by-negotiating-salary, accessed 2026-09-04).

## 2. Anchoring with a documented range

Our documented ranges (content/research-facts.md): clinical liaison $84k to $135k; customer success $75k to $120k; clinical informatics $97.8k to $154k; project management $85k to $100k+; content $80k to $141k; data $70k to $105k; instructional design $70k to $100k. The lesson should teach the reader to name a range from this file, not their clinical salary, because the former is the market for the role and the latter anchors low.

When asked for expectations, give a range whose bottom is the number you would actually accept; when asked for current salary, in most states you may decline and, in the states below, the employer may not ask.

Salary-history bans (statewide, private employers; HR Dive tracker updated 2026-04-28, https://www.hrdive.com/news/salary-history-ban-states-list/516662/, accessed 2026-09-04): Alabama, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Missouri (6+ employees), Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia (effective 2026-07-01), Washington, plus Puerto Rico. North Carolina and Pennsylvania restrict only state agencies. Many cities add their own.

Pay range required in job postings (Rippling guide updated 2026-05-12, https://www.rippling.com/blog/pay-transparency-laws-state-by-state-guide, accessed 2026-09-04): California (2023-01-01, 15+ employees), Colorado (posting rule effective 2021-01-01, amended 2024; TheComplyGuide, https://thecomplyguide.com/pay-transparency-laws-by-state-2026-map/, accessed 2026-09-04), Washington (2023-01-01, 15+), New York (2023-09-17, 4+), Hawaii (2024-01-01, 50+), Maryland (2024-10-01, 15+), District of Columbia (2024-06-30), Illinois (2025-01-01, 15+), Minnesota (2025-01-01, 30+), New Jersey (2025-06-01, 10+), Vermont (2025-07-01, 5+), Massachusetts (2025-10-29, 25+). Connecticut, Nevada and Rhode Island require the range on request or before an offer. Trackers disagree on the count (Paycor lists 18 states plus D.C. with some form of transparency law, https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/pay-transparency-laws-by-state/, accessed 2026-09-04), so the lesson should say "check your state" and link the tracker rather than promise a number. Remote postings usually fall under the strictest state where the job could be done.

## 3. Total compensation for these roles

Customer success. Practitioner guidance puts CSM pay at 70/30 to 80/20 base-to-variable of on-target earnings (ClientSuccess, no survey, https://www.clientsuccess.com/resources/compensation-plans-for-customer-success-managers, accessed 2026-09-04). The one cited data point is the 2024 Customer Success Salary survey (community-sourced, 200+ submissions): about 83% base and 17% variable in the U.S. (via Everstage, https://www.everstage.com/sales-compensation/csm-variable-compensation, accessed 2026-09-04). Gainsight's 2023 survey (1,434 respondents, North America and Europe) found 84% of CSMs compensated on renewal performance and 66% on expansion revenue (https://communities.gainsight.com/deliver-customer-success-9/customer-success-compensation-2023-report-released-16775, accessed 2026-09-04). Practical rule: ask what the variable is tied to and what share of the team hit target last year.

Sales. The Bridge Group's 2024 SaaS AE benchmark (170+ B2B SaaS companies) reports median OTE $190k and a 53:47 base-to-variable split (https://blog.bridgegroupinc.com/2024-ae-metrics-compensation-benchmark, accessed 2026-09-04). An OTE is not a salary; the base is what is guaranteed.

Equity. A grant is a right to shares that you earn over time (vesting). Stripe Atlas describes the standard: a four-year vesting period with a one-year cliff, meaning no equity vests until you have been there a full year, then 25% vests on the cliff date and the rest monthly (1/48 of the grant per month) (https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/equity-terms, accessed 2026-09-04). Leave before the cliff and you keep nothing. Startup equity is a lottery ticket, not income; the lesson should treat it as zero when comparing offers.

Sign-on bonuses. WorldatWork's 2024 report found 80% of organizations use sign-on bonuses and 98% use some bonus program (sample not disclosed; https://worldatwork.org/about/press-room/new-report-highlights-growing-use-of-bonus-programs-across-organizations, accessed 2026-09-04). ZipRecruiter's Q4 2025 hires survey put the share of new hires actually receiving one at 15%, the lowest of 2025 (reported via https://x.com/HedgieMarkets/status/2018113100764106891, accessed 2026-09-04; primary at https://www.ziprecruiter-research.org/new-hires-survey). A sign-on is the easiest yes when the base is capped, because it does not compound into the salary band.

Non-cash items. Robert Half's 2019 survey (1,000+ workers, 1,000+ CFOs) found 43% of workers had rejected or lost interest in an offer because the employer would not negotiate beyond salary; 45% of CFOs said they would negotiate remote work or scheduling, 47% benefits, 52% professional development (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/survey-2-in-5-workers-lose-interest-in-job-offer-if-employer-wont-negotiate-beyond-salary-300953598.html, accessed 2026-09-04). Marks and Harold found collaborative negotiators picked up "more vacation, better healthcare, or help with education expenses" alongside modest salary gains (strategy-business, above). PTO, start date, remote days, and a six-month review date are all standard asks.

## 4. The pivot-role trade: what the evidence says

The evidence on career changers' pay trajectory is thin and mostly survey-based. What exists:

Pay cuts on a switch are common in a soft market: 27% of new hires in Q4 2025 took a cut, and 56% got a raise (ZipRecruiter n=1,500, Yahoo/The Hill above). Prudential's 2022 Pulse survey found one-third of pandemic job switchers took less pay for work-life balance (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2022/03/14/one-third-of-job-switchers-took-a-pay-cut-for-better-work-life-balance.html, accessed 2026-09-04; sample not recovered). A Rutgers study reported in the New York Times (2011) found around 70% of workers who changed fields after unemployment took a pay cut (Cornell ILR summary, https://catherwood.library.cornell.edu/wit/career-shift-often-means-drop-in-living-standards, accessed 2026-09-04).

Switching per se is not a wage penalty. Pew's analysis of CPS data (April 2021 to March 2022) found 60% of job changers saw real wage gains versus 47% of stayers, median +9.7% versus -1.7%, and about half of job changers also changed occupation (https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2022/07/28/majority-of-u-s-workers-changing-jobs-are-seeing-real-wage-gains/, accessed 2026-09-04). That was a hot market, and it does not isolate first-time career changers.

Indeed's 2019 Career Change Report found 58% of U.S. workers willing to take a pay cut to change careers and 88% of changers happier afterward (sample not given; https://www.indeed.com/lead/career-change via https://www.careershifters.org/career-change-statistics?field_country_focus_value=US, accessed 2026-09-04). No longitudinal dataset was found that tracks earnings recovery for voluntary career changers specifically; claims like "recover within 18 to 36 months" circulate on career blogs without a traceable source and should not be used.

## 5. Scripts

Expectations. "From what I've seen for this role, the range runs about $84,000 to $135,000 depending on territory and company. Given my clinical background and what this job needs, I'm looking at the middle of that. Where does this role sit?" (Swap in the documented range for the path.)

Countering. "Thank you, I'm excited about this. Based on the range I've seen for this work, I was expecting closer to $92,000. Is there room to get there on base, or on a sign-on bonus if the base is fixed?" (Name one number, slightly above the target; ask about the second lever in the same breath.)

Time. "I'd like to review the full offer, benefits and all. Can I get back to you by Thursday?" (Two to three business days is normal; Salary.com's employer sample said 84% expect negotiation, so a short pause is not a red flag.)

## 6. What to do with a lowball

Decision rule, in order: (1) Is the offer inside the documented range for the role? If it is below the bottom, counter to the bottom with the range as the reason. (2) Does it clear the runway number from the money lesson? An offer below runway is a no unless something else changes. (3) Does the role have a ladder? Ask what people in this seat moved to, and when.

When below-clinical is still right: the research-coordinator springboard. BLS puts clinical research coordinators at $48k to $72k (25th to 75th percentile; median $57,510), below where most SLPs start, and the only documented path where a cut is the expected outcome (content/research-facts.md). Two SLPs in our file used it as a step: one went CF to university study coordinator (about 18 months) to pharma patient safety at +$10k base plus a $5k bonus, then +$15k eighteen months later. Take that trade only if the runway covers the gap and the next move is named before you sign.

## What the lesson can say with confidence

1. Most people do not negotiate: about 30% did in both Pew's 2023 sample and ZipRecruiter's Q4 2025 sample.
2. Most who ask get something: roughly two-thirds in Pew, 85% to 91% in Fidelity and ZipRecruiter.
3. The best field study of new hires found about $5,000 more a year for those who negotiated.
4. Offers are almost never withdrawn for negotiating: hiring managers report fewer than 2 withdrawals across ~27 offers, and 84% of employers expect a counter.
5. Candidates overestimate the risk of asking, and priming them with the facts does not fully fix it.
6. In at least twelve states plus D.C. the range must be posted; in at least twenty the employer cannot ask salary history.
7. Customer success pay is mostly base (about 83/17 in the one cited survey); sales is closer to half variable.
8. Startup equity vests over four years with a one-year cliff; leave early and it is worth nothing.
9. Sign-on bonuses are common tools but reached only 15% of new hires in late 2025, so ask.
10. PTO, remote days, and start date are routinely negotiated, and collaborative asks tend to win them.

## What it must hedge

- The "$5,000" and "7.4%" gains come from two samples (149 new hires; one cohort of Carnegie Mellon master's grads) and are not role-specific.
- Rescission data comes from employer self-report; employees in the 2013 Salary.com survey reported losing offers at 19%, and a 2024 survey claim of "fewer than 2%" of managers could not be traced to a source.
- Pay-transparency and salary-history lists change several times a year and trackers disagree on counts; link a tracker, do not hard-code the list in the lesson.
- CS base/variable norms rest on practitioner guidance and a 200-submission community dataset, not a probability sample.
- There is no longitudinal evidence on how fast first-time career changers recover a pay cut; the springboard case is two documented individuals.
- All survey figures are self-reported and skew toward U.S. white-collar respondents; none sample SLPs.
