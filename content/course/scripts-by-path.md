# Scripts by path

Working copy for Transition OS. Every script is second person, plain, and
built for an SLP moving into one of the eleven paths on the map. Brackets are
the parts you fill in. Nothing here carries a statistic; the numbers belong in
the lessons, where they have a source under them.

Where each one goes in the course is noted in italics. Several are already
live (3.1, 3.2, 3.7, 6.2, 6.5, 6.6, 6.8); the rest are ready to drop in.

---

## 1. The sentence for the staff room

*Lesson 1.7. Already live.*

> "I'm looking at what else I could do with my SLP background."

If they ask what: "I don't know yet. I'm finding out." True, and it ends the
conversation on your terms.

---

## 2. The warm ask, to someone you already know outside speech

*Lesson 3.1. Live.*

> I'm looking at [target title] roles, and I know you've been at [company]
> a while. Could you tell me what the [title] there actually does day to day?
> And if there's someone doing it you'd trust me to talk to, I'd love fifteen
> minutes with them.
>
> No job ask. I'm working out whether it fits before I go further.

When they can't help: "Who do you know who works somewhere interesting?" The
second name is usually the one that matters.

## 3. Sixty-second pitches, one per path

*For lesson 3.2 and the "tell me about yourself" opener in 6.1. Say the
years, the two things you'd do all day, the title, the ask.*

**Clinical liaison**
> I've spent [N] years as a medical SLP, most of it in [acute / inpatient
> rehab / SNF], where half my week was case conferences, discharge planning
> and explaining to families and physicians why a patient did or didn't
> qualify for the next level of care. I'm moving into clinical liaison work,
> where that's the whole job. If you know a rehab facility or home health
> agency that's adding a liaison, I'd like to hear about it.

**Customer success or implementation, health-tech**
> I'm an SLP with [N] years in [setting]. The part of the job I'd keep is
> onboarding: getting a family or a teacher to actually use the device or the
> plan after I leave the room. I'm moving into customer success at a
> health-tech or teletherapy company, where that's the work all day. If your
> team is hiring an implementation or onboarding specialist, I'd like to talk.

**Utilization review**
> [N] years as an SLP, and the part I'm good at is the part most people hate:
> reading the chart, matching what was done against what the payer will
> cover, and writing the justification that survives an audit. I'm moving into
> utilization review. If you know a health plan or a UR vendor that hires
> therapists into reviewer roles, I'd welcome an introduction.

**Clinical informatics**
> I've been the SLP who ends up fixing the documentation system. [N] years in
> [setting], superuser through [EMR name] rollout, built the note templates
> the department still uses. I'm moving into clinical informatics, the analyst
> or trainer side, where I translate between the clinicians and the people
> building the system. If your organisation has an EHR analyst or trainer
> opening, I'd like to be considered.

**Clinical educator or trainer, device and speech-tech**
> [N] years as an SLP, and the work I loved most was teaching other
> clinicians to use [AAC / a speaking valve / a dysphagia protocol]. I've
> trained [teams / a district / nursing staff] on it. I'm moving into a
> clinical educator role at a company that makes the products, where teaching
> clinicians is the job. If you know one that's hiring, I'd like to talk.

**Instructional design**
> I'm an SLP with [N] years in [setting]. I've built training for adults the
> whole time: in-services, onboarding for CFs, a [program] the department
> still runs. I've rebuilt one of them as a [Rise / Storyline] module so you
> can see how I work. I'm moving into instructional design. If your team
> needs someone who can design for adults who are busy and sceptical, I'd like
> to show you the module.

**Healthcare data and analytics**
> [N] years as an SLP, and I'm the one who kept the outcome data nobody asked
> for: [dismissal rates / wait times / goal attainment] by [setting], in a
> spreadsheet that answered questions a year later. I'm moving into healthcare
> analytics, and I've been learning [SQL / Tableau] to do it properly. If your
> quality or analytics team has an analyst opening, I'd welcome a conversation.

**Project or program management**
> For [N] years I've run programs that had a deadline, a budget and a dozen
> people who didn't report to me: [a caseload of 60 across three buildings /
> a dysphagia program / an EMR transition]. I'm moving into project
> management in healthcare, and I'm working through [the Google PM
> certificate / CAPM]. If your organisation has a coordinator or associate PM
> role, I'd like to be considered.

**Content and marketing, health**
> I'm an SLP with [N] years of explaining hard clinical ideas to people who
> didn't ask for a glossary: families, teachers, nurses. I've started writing
> that down: [a blog / three samples / a newsletter]. I'm moving into health
> content and marketing, where translation is the whole job. If your team
> needs a writer who has actually done the clinical work, I'd like to send
> you the samples.

**Medical and device sales**
> [N] years as an SLP. Every week I sold a treatment plan to a family that
> didn't want to hear it and a protocol to nurses who were too busy for it,
> and most of them came round. I'm moving into clinical sales for a [device /
> health-tech] company, where that's the work with a quota attached. If your
> sales team is adding a clinical specialist, I'd like to hear about it.

**Rehab management**
> I've been an SLP in [setting] for [N] years and I already run the parts of
> the department nobody bills for: the schedule, the coverage, the audits, the
> new grad who's drowning. I'm looking to move into a clinical manager or
> program manager role, here or somewhere like here. Who did the last two
> openings go to, and what did they have that I don't yet?

## 4. The first message to a stranger who made your move

*Lesson 3.2. Live. Under ninety words, one fixed ask, easy to decline.*

> Hi [Name], I'm an SLP in [setting] and I'm looking at [target title] roles.
> I found you because you made the same move, and I'd rather ask you than
> read another article about it.
>
> Would you be up for fifteen minutes in the next couple of weeks? I mostly
> want to know how you got the first one and what you'd do differently. No
> job ask, I promise.
>
> Either way, thank you for putting your path somewhere someone could find it.

The follow-up, once, seven to ten days later. Then stop.

## 5. The message that doesn't get answered, and why

*Lesson 3.2. Show it beside the one above.*

> Hi! I'm a speech pathologist looking to leave the field. I saw you're a
> Customer Success Manager. Could you tell me what a typical project looks
> like from start to finish, how you got the job, what the interview was like,
> what the salary is, and whether they're hiring? Also, would you be willing
> to look at my résumé?

Five questions, two hours of work for a stranger, a résumé attached, and a
title they'd have to guess. If Google could answer it, don't ask it.

## 6. The note to a hiring manager when a role is live

*Lesson 3.7. One line of proof, one ask.*

> Hi [Name], does the [title] role posted on [LinkedIn / your careers page]
> report to you? I've spent [N] years as an SLP in [setting], and [one
> specific, verifiable thing: trained fourteen clinicians through an EMR
> rollout / ran a caseload of 62 across three sites / built the dysphagia
> protocol the unit still uses]. I've applied. If you're the person hiring
> for it, I'd like the chance to talk.

---

## 7. The comeback when a recruiter says you're not qualified

*Lesson 3.7.*

> Thanks for being straight with me. I don't have [Salesforce / Articulate /
> SQL] yet. If I complete [a named course] before the interview date, would
> you be comfortable putting me forward? My [N] years in [setting] cover
> [the two requirements you do meet], and I learn tools fast because I've had
> to.
>
> If the answer is still no, I'd value one line on what would change it, and
> when it's reasonable to check back.

## 8. The cover letter, in four moves

*Lesson 4.6. The test: if any SLP could have written it, rewrite it.*

1. **Who you are, for this role.** Your pitch from section 3, cut to two
   sentences.
2. **Why this role, at this company.** One specific thing about them you
   couldn't say about a competitor. One sentence.
3. **Three things you've done that match three lines of the posting.** Each
   with a number.
4. **The switch, in one sentence, positive.** "I'm moving from clinical work
   into [role] because [the pull]." Then the close.

**Sample, customer success at a teletherapy platform**

> I'm an SLP with eight years in schools, the last three of them as the
> person who onboarded every new family and every new teacher onto [the
> platform we used], and I'd like to do that work for [Company] full time.
>
> I've used [Company's product] with [N] students and I know where families
> get stuck in the first two weeks, because I was the one they called.
>
> Three things from the posting I've already done: I trained [N] teachers and
> aides on a new documentation system in one semester; I kept a caseload of
> [N] families engaged through a remote year, with [N] percent still
> attending in May; and I built the home-program handouts my district still
> sends out.
>
> I'm moving from clinical work into customer success because the part of the
> job I'd keep is the onboarding, and here it's the whole job. My résumé is
> attached, and I'd welcome a conversation.
>
> [Your name]

## 9. "Tell me about yourself"

*Lesson 6.1. Forty-five seconds. Pitch, then pull, then one number.*

> I've been an SLP for [N] years, mostly in [setting]. The part I've been
> best at, and the part I want to do all day, is [the two things]. About a
> year ago I started [the preparation: the course, the module, the
> conversations], and I applied here because [the specific thing about the
> role]. The number I'm proudest of is [one accomplishment with a figure].

## 10. "Why are you leaving clinical work?"

*Lesson 6.2. The bridge builder is live. These are three shapes that answer
without handing over the retention doubt.*

**The pull**
> I want to work on the systems that decide how [patients / students] get
> served, rather than absorbing the consequences one session at a time. This
> role is exactly that.

**The scale**
> I've helped [N] families one at a time for [N] years. I want the next [N]
> years to reach more people than I can fit on a caseload, and [role] is how
> that happens.

**The part you'd keep**
> The thing I'd keep from clinical work is [teaching clinicians / reading the
> chart / getting people to use the plan], and in this role that's the whole
> job instead of a corner of it.

If a version contains burnout, paperwork, productivity, caseload size or
administration, rewrite that sentence. All true; all a gift to the doubt.

## 11. Salary, plan A and plan B

*Lesson 6.5. Live. Plan A deflects once; plan B has the floor ready, because
phone screens ask anyway.*

**Plan A**
> I'm focused on finding the right fit, and I've seen the range for this role
> sits around [$X to $Y]. I'd rather hear what you've budgeted before I
> anchor on a number.

**Plan B, when they insist**
> Based on the range for [title] in [city / remote], I'm looking at [$X to
> $Y]. I care about the whole package, so if the base sits at the lower end
> I'd want to talk about [remote days / PTO / an early review]. What range did
> you have in mind?

**The floor, if you must**
> It's negotiable, and I'm focused on the fit. I couldn't go below [$X] on
> base, because that's what my [loans / household] need to work.

## 12. The counter, likeable

*Lesson 6.5. Live.*

> Thank you, I'm glad we got here. I'd like to accept at [$Y]. That's inside
> the range you posted, and it reflects [the clinical background the role
> asks for / the systems experience / the licence]. If the base is fixed,
> could we look at [sign-on / an early review / extra PTO]?

Not: "I know my worth." Same request, different room.

## 13. Five lines for the middle of an interview

*Lesson 6.3.*

**A term you don't know**
> I'm not familiar with that one. Is it similar to [the closest thing you do
> know]?

**Nerves showing**
> Sorry, I'm excited about this role and my nerves are getting ahead of me.
> Let me start that again.

**A skill you don't have yet**
> I haven't used [tool] in a job. I've [taken the course / built one thing in
> it], and I've picked up [EMR / a documentation system / a scheduling
> platform] on the job every time I've had to.

**The out-of-left-field question**
Answer it like a person. They're checking whether you're one.

**The last question, only if you can hear the answer**
> Is there anything you've seen so far that would make you hesitate to hire
> me for this?

## 14. Eager, not desperate

*Lesson 6.1.*

Desperate:
> Honestly I'd take anything that isn't a caseload. I'm so done.

Eager:
> I learned [the thing] in clinical work, and this is the role where I get to
> use it all day. It's the right fit because [A], [B] and [C].

Desperate closes with "when will I hear?" Eager closes with:
> What's your timeline for the other candidates, and is there anything else
> I can send you in the meantime?

## 15. "May we contact your current employer?"

*Lesson 6.6. Live.*

> Not my current one, please, while I'm still there. I can put you in touch
> with [a past supervisor / a mentor / a colleague who's since moved on], all
> of whom supervised or worked alongside me directly.

## 16. "When can you start?", under a school contract

*Lesson 6.5.*

> My contract runs to [date], and I intend to honour it. I can start [the
> Monday after]. If the role needs someone sooner, I'd want to understand the
> timeline before I decide anything, because I'd rather tell you a real date
> than a hopeful one.

Decide before the interview whether breaking the contract is on the table.
Hesitation in the room reads as doubt about the role.

*Lesson 6.6. Live.*

> anything else that would help you decide, tell me and it's yours.
>
> [Your name]

## 19. Answering a no from a company you liked

*Lesson 6.8. Live.*

> Thank you for letting me know, and for the time your team gave me. I
> enjoyed the conversations about [specific thing], and I'd still like to
> work with you. If a similar role opens in the next few months, I'd welcome
> the chance to be considered, and I'll keep an eye on the careers page.
>
> [Your name]

- Clinical Educator · AAC and dysphagia training for clinicians · [N] teams trained
