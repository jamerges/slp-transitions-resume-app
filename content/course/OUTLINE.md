# Transition OS: the course. Outline v1 (2026-09-03, for James's approval)

Working title: **Transition OS: the 90-day guided exit from clinical SLP work**
(Alternative titles: "The Exit Plan", "Out, Not Over". Decide after reading.)

## What it is, in one paragraph
A 90-day, self-paced program that takes an SLP from "I want out and feel guilty about it" to a signed offer in a documented non-clinical path, without starting over. Six modules, 38 bite-sized lessons (5 to 12 minutes each), each with a short video, an animated explainer or interactive tool, one action step, and a downloadable resource. Progress is tracked like a quest log. Every claim in it traces to content/research-facts.md and the stories on the site, so it says things the $599 competitor courses cannot: real SLP salary bands, real timelines, and the three factual guardrails (Epic sponsorship, MSL doctorate, UXR saturation).

## Learning design (the LMS practices, applied)
- **Bite-size by default.** No lesson over 12 minutes. Each lesson has exactly one job, one action, one resource.
- **Progress you can see.** A module map (quest log) with a bar per module, a checkmark per lesson, and a "you are here" marker. Overall completion percentage on every screen.
- **Momentum mechanics, tied to real work, not clicks.** XP for completing an *action step* (not for watching). Badges for artifacts: "First Translation" (one résumé bullet rewritten), "First Reach-Out" (one message sent), "Proof Artifact" (one thing you made), "Application 1", "Interview 1", "Offer". A streak counter for days with an action, with a grace day so a missed Tuesday doesn't reset it (loss aversion works against you if it's cruel).
- **Checkpoints, not tests.** Each module ends with a two-minute self-check that unlocks the next module. Nothing is graded. Every checkpoint feeds the AI coach so its answers stay specific to that person.
- **Spaced return.** Email and optional SMS nudges at day 2, 5, 9, then weekly, each carrying the next single action, not a summary. Silent when the person is on streak.
- **Stage-aware entry.** The quiz's stage question sets the starting module: stages 1–2 begin at Module 1, stage 3 at Module 2, stage 4 at Module 3, stage 5 skips to Module 5 with Module 4 flagged as "come back for this."
- **Cohort feel without a cohort.** A monthly "start line" date so buyers begin together, a shared anonymous progress wall ("31 people finished Module 2 this week"), and one live Q&A per month (you, 45 minutes, recorded into the course). This is the cheapest way to borrow cohort completion rates (about 64% vs 48% self-paced in the market data).
- **Certificate of completion**, not CEUs. Honest wording: "completed the Transition OS program".

## The modules

### Module 0: Start here (15 minutes total)
0.1 Welcome from James (video, 3 min): what this is, what it isn't, the refund promise, how to use the quest log.
0.2 Your starting line (interactive, 5 min): stage check, path result imported from the quiz or set here, income floor, hours available, target date. Generates the personal 90-day map.
0.3 The rules of the road (animated, 4 min): the three lies ("start over", "need a certificate first", "I'd be throwing away my degree") and the three guardrails (Epic, MSL, UXR).
Resource: the 90-day map (auto-generated), the workbook (DOCX).

### Module 1: Ground. Permission, identity, and the honest decision (Week 1)
1.1 You're allowed to want out (video, 6 min): the five stages, where you are, what moves you.
1.2 Bad workplace, bad fit, bad season (interactive decision tree, 8 min): the framework from the site, made clickable. Ends with a written verdict.
1.3 The sunk-cost audit (animated + worksheet, 7 min): degree grief and the loans, with the numbers. What's spent, what's still on the table.
1.4 What you're protecting (interactive, 8 min): five dials (pay floor, hours, clinical proximity, people-time, tech appetite) that set which paths fit. Our answer to the "archetype" quizzes: it lands on real paths and real pay.
1.5 Identity continuity (video, 5 min): you are still the person who explains hard things simply. Three stories, thirty seconds each.
1.6 Tell one person (action, 3 min): the smallest possible disclosure. Badge: "Said it out loud."
Checkpoint: verdict + dials + one-sentence "why I'm leaving" in pull language.
Resources: the decision worksheet, the sunk-cost calculator (spreadsheet), the five-fears reading.

### Module 2: Explore. The twenty paths and the one that fits (Weeks 2–3)
2.1 The map (animated, 9 min): twenty paths on one screen, sorted by timeline (weeks / 6–12 months / 12–24 months), each with its sourced range and its catch.
2.2 The fast exits (video, 8 min): clinical liaison, utilization review, clinical educator, case management. What the day looks like, who hires, the licence-as-credential point.
2.3 The most-travelled roads (video, 9 min): customer success, project management, healthcare data, content. Real outcomes with the quotes ("I'm making 20k more a year base", "no one took me seriously with a CAPM").
2.4 The long builds (video, 7 min): informatics, instructional design, UX, software, conversation design. What they cost in months and what they pay.
2.5 The three guardrails, in full (animated, 6 min): why Epic can't be bought, why MSL needs a doctorate, why UXR is oversaturated. What to do instead in each case.
2.6 Pick one (interactive, 8 min): the Pivot Report, generated inside the course from your résumé. Top three paths, entry doors, readiness.
2.7 Path deep-dive (choose your own, 10 min): one lesson per path, unlocked for your top two. Written by path, with a named employer list from the companies database and this week's live postings from the job digest.
2.8 Informational interviews that get answered (video + scripts, 9 min): the message that gets a one-in-four reply rate, what to ask, what to do after.
2.9 Reach out to three people (action, 20 min over the week). Badge: "First Reach-Out." Contact tracker included.
Checkpoint: one chosen path, one runner-up, three messages sent.
Resources: path cards (PDF per path), the companies database, the message scripts, the contact tracker (spreadsheet), the salary table with sources.

### Module 3: Translate. Your experience in the buyer's language (Weeks 3–5)
3.1 The seven-second problem (animated, 5 min): what a recruiter sees, the 7.4-second first pass, the two piles.
3.2 The translation layer (video, 10 min): caseload → portfolio, IEP → stakeholder alignment, chart audits → quality assurance, parent buy-in → objection handling. The full pairs list, with the "chef listing food" warning.
3.3 Numbers you already have (worksheet, 8 min): pull every real number out of your clinical work (62 students, 14 teachers trained, one EMR rollout survived). No number, no bullet.
3.4 The AI trap (video, 6 min): 62% reject unpersonalized AI applications; the tell-words; how to use the tools without sounding like the other 34 applicants.
3.5 Build your résumé in the Suite (interactive, 15 min): the Career Pivot Suite, included. Paste résumé and one real posting, get every bullet translated, refine six sections.
3.6 LinkedIn as the background check (video + checklist, 8 min): headline, About, experience rewritten; the recruiter search terms for your path.
3.7 The cover letter as costly signal (video, 6 min): the structure, pull not push, one paragraph only you could write.
3.8 Send one application properly (action, 30 min). Badge: "Application 1."
Checkpoint: translated résumé, LinkedIn live, one tailored application sent.
Resources: translation pairs (PDF), the number-mining worksheet, LinkedIn checklist, cover-letter skeleton, banned-words list.

### Module 4: Test. Proof artifacts and the bridge (Weeks 5–8)
4.1 Why proof beats certificates (video, 6 min): the CAPM quote, portfolio-over-credential in instructional design, the "one honest case study" line from UX.
4.2 The proof-artifact menu (interactive, 8 min): one small artifact per path (a Rise module, a one-page EMR rollout story, a spreadsheet with a pivot table, a published explainer, a rebuilt training). Pick one, get the brief.
4.3 Make it in a week (action, 2–4 hours over the week). Badge: "Proof Artifact."
4.4 The bridge options (video, 7 min): part-time telepractice, PRN, contract work, a side project. How to buy yourself hours without buying yourself a pay cut.
4.5 Money and the bridge (video + calculator, 9 min): runway math, the honest timelines (6–15 months), what a temporary dip actually costs against a $20k raise later. **Research required before writing: PSLF and student-loan implications of leaving clinical employment.** This is the single biggest unwritten reader question and it needs sourced facts, not opinion.
4.6 The referral engine (video, 7 min): why 500 cold applications is the tax for having no referrals; the 48-hour posting rule; how to convert a conversation into an internal referral.
4.7 Keep two conversations warm (action, ongoing). Badge: "Warm Network."
Checkpoint: one artifact linked, bridge decision written, runway number known.
Resources: artifact briefs (per path), runway calculator, PSLF explainer (after research), bridge-role postings from the job digest.

### Module 5: Leap. Interviews, offers, and the first 90 days (Weeks 8–12)
5.1 How career changers get screened (video, 7 min): retention doubt, jargon they can't map, no proof of investment. The three objections and the three answers.
5.2 Bridge statements (video + script builder, 9 min): "why are you leaving clinical work" in pull language, practiced until it's boring.
5.3 Screening questions that filter out career changers (interactive, 8 min): the questions, the answers the Suite generates, the ones to write yourself.
5.4 The mock interview (interactive, 15 min): the AI coach runs a 10-question interview for your path and gives feedback grounded in the research. Repeatable.
5.5 Salary and the offer (video, 8 min): the ranges by path, how to anchor, what to do with a lowball, when the pay cut is acceptable (research coordinator as a springboard) and when it isn't. **Research required: negotiation outcomes for clinicians moving to industry.**
5.6 Volume without despair (video, 6 min): 113 applications, 7 interviews, 1 offer. Rejection as a volume statistic. The weekly numbers to track.
5.7 The first 90 days in the new role (video, 7 min): the clinician habits that help, the ones to drop, how to bank the first win.
5.8 Accept, or keep going (action). Badge: "Offer." Certificate unlocks.
Checkpoint: bridge statement recorded, one mock interview completed, tracking sheet live.
Resources: interview question bank by path, bridge-statement builder, application tracker, offer checklist, the 90-day plan from the Suite.

### Module 6: After (unlocked at completion, and on its own for alumni)
6.1 Tell your story (action): the story form on /about, or a podcast conversation. Alumni become the next cohort's proof.
6.2 The next rung (video, 4 min): what changes at year one, when to move again, what leadership and management paths look like from inside.
6.3 Pay it forward (optional): answer one message from someone in Module 2.

## Research changes (2026-09-04, from content/voc-2026-09-forums.md, 101 threads)
- **Lead with permission.** The buyer's first question is "am I allowed to want out?", and the answer has to come from someone who has clearly been an SLP. Module 1 stays first; the sample now carries one verbatim forum quote per stage.
- **Name the settings experiment.** "Have you tried a different setting" is the reply every exit thread gets. Lesson 1.2 now says so and treats a third setting change as data.
- **Money in the syllabus.** Loans and PSLF timing, the pay-cut budget, COBRA, and when to drop the C's are the loudest unmet need in the corpus. Lesson 4.5 grows into two lessons: "The loan is a calendar" (PSLF count, qualifying employers, timing the exit) and "The bridge budget" (pay cut, COBRA, the first pivot role's pay). Research before writing.
- **The artifact people beg for is a real before-and-after résumé.** A sample rewritten résumé draws more replies than any salary figure. Lesson 3.5 must show one, line by line, before the Suite builds theirs.
- **Salary parity goes in the first line of the promise.** It is the number-one priority in the only large quantitative sample in the corpus (about 4,000 PT/OT/SLP quiz respondents at The Non-Clinical PT) and the loudest UK complaint.
- **Three moves that need no new degree or certificate**, shown early: liaison, utilization review, clinical educator. "I can't retrain" kills the sale before price does.
- **Pricing, honestly.** Stage-3/4 readers say they won't pay $400+ to be pointed somewhere; $9 to $129 is bought on trust plus a 30-day refund; $399 to $599 is bought with buy-now-pay-later after reading reviews; four-digit coaching only after "a hard conversation" with a spouse. So: the $9 report and $24 suite stay as the entry rungs, the course sells to stage 4/5, the checkout offers instalments, and the 30-day refund sits on the button. The $497 list holds; expect most sales at the $297 launch price and from people who already bought the $24 product.
- **Give them a two-sentence reply** to "you're making the shortage worse" and "just change settings". Lesson 1.6 now has both.
- **Distribution:** r/SLPcareertransitions and the teacher-transition Facebook groups hold the stage-4 readers; r/slp bans promotion; LinkedIn carries no SLP reader voice. Testimonials and DM-style Q&A will outperform ads.
- **Use their numbers in the copy:** 85–95% productivity, caseloads of 75 to 186, 14 sessions a day, $200k loans, 500 applications, 15 months.

## Review round 1 (James, 2026-09-04) and what changed
- **Buttons.** One label per action, and it never changes after completion: "Save my starting line", "Save my numbers", "Save these three to my map", "Mark as watched". Completion shows as a green checkmark line next to the button, not as a renamed button. Every lesson ends with the same two buttons, "← Quest log" and "Next lesson →", with the next title in small text underneath.
- **Path chosen vs. "Not sure yet".** The path is optional and only personalises. Every principle lesson (mindset, résumé, LinkedIn, networking, interviews) is written for any title. A chosen path changes four things: which examples and quotes appear, which deep-dive lesson unlocks in Module 2 (2.7), which live postings and artifact brief you get (2.7, 4.2), and the default target in the money calculators. "Not sure yet" hides nothing; Module 2 chooses with you via the dials and the Pivot Report, and you can change the path at any time from the starting line.
- **Guardrails.** Epic sponsorship, the MSL doctorate gate and UX-research saturation are path facts, not mindset, so they left Module 0. Lesson 0.3 is now "Three things I believed" (start over, certificate first, wasting the degree), three minutes, and the guardrails live in Module 2, lesson 5, next to the paths they belong to.
