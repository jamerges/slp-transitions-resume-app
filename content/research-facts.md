# Research Facts for SLP Transitions Content

Verified facts from July 2026 research (SLP community archives, hiring-side sources, competitor analysis). Writers: cite ONLY facts from this file or the style guide — never invent numbers, salaries, or success stories. Where a range is given, keep the range.

## Where SLPs actually land (by documented success frequency)

**Tier 1 (most real success stories):**
- **Project/Program Management** — 6-figure outcomes documented (one IT PM at 6 figures after 15 months; associate PM after 1 year). Path: Google PM cert (free-low cost) → CAPM ($175) → PMP ($400+$139 PMI membership). Caution from a real transitioner: "no one took me seriously with a CAPM" — certs open doors only combined with translated experience. Clinical coordination hours can legitimately count toward PMP's experience requirement.
- **Healthcare Data Analyst** — real outcomes: fully remote analyst after 500+ apps over 6 months (pay above SLP salary); analyst at behavioral health org after a 1-yr online MS; "I'm making 20k more a year base than I ever did as an SLP." Entry: healthcare orgs (falls data, discharge follow-up), NOT big tech. Google Data Analytics cert or DataCamp route works.
- **Customer Success / Implementation / Training (health-tech, SaaS, edtech)** — best effort-to-odds ratio. Real outcome: medical SLP → remote SaaS manager at $115-120k via SV Academy's free CS bootcamp + paid internship. Range $75-120k. At speech-tech companies (Expressable, Presence, Tobii Dynavox, PRC-Saltillo, Lingraphica, Passy-Muir) the SLP license itself is the credential. Entry doors: Implementation Specialist / Onboarding Manager before CSM.
- **Marketing / Copywriting / Content** — corporate marketing after ~1 year of applications ("I actually got a raise and bonus this year, whereas in my 5 years in healthcare I was given a .35 cent raise"); freelance healthcare copywriter started with one $200 Upwork article, now runs a micro-agency. Range $80k-141k.

**Tier 2 (fast, clinical-adjacent):**
- **Clinical/Rehab Liaison** — $84k-135k, clinical credential is sufficient, called "the #1 non-clinical title for rehab professionals." Employers: Encompass Health, Select Medical, Lifepoint.
- **Utilization Review** — $80-88k ($37-62/hr), remote-heavy, fast entry. Also state Disability Determination Services (one SLP took a $13k pay cut for mobility + PSLF — tradeoffs are real).
- **Clinical Educator at device companies** — Tobii Dynavox "Learning Consultant" $78-116k (Glassdoor). Also PRC-Saltillo, Lingraphica, Passy-Muir.
- **Clinical Research / Study Coordinator (the stepping-stone path)** — $48k-72k (BLS OEWS 25th-75th percentile; median $57,510). Self-report aggregators run higher (Payscale ~$58.6k, ZipRecruiter ~$59.7k, Indeed ~$74k) — use the BLS band, it's the honest one. **CRITICAL FRAMING: this is the only path here where a pay cut is the expected outcome, not a risk** — every other documented path starts at $70k+. Its value is as a first step out, not a destination. Universities and academic medical centers hire varied backgrounds, entry is fast (weeks to a couple of months, no new credential), and benefits are usually strong while pay is not. Two documented SLPs used it exactly as a springboard: one went CF → university study coordinator (~18 months) → pharma patient safety/pharmacovigilance for +$10k base and a $5k bonus, then +$15k 18 months after that; another went school SLP → university research coordinator. Never present this as a destination role.
- **Clinical Informatics / EHR Analyst** — $97.8k-154k. CRITICAL FACT: Epic certification CANNOT be self-obtained — employer sponsorship only. Correct path: sponsor-track junior analyst roles, go-live/activation support gigs, Epic consulting firms (Nordic, Tegria). Self-serve credential: CAHIMS (HIMSS, designed for career changers, no experience prerequisite). Real example: an SLP became an Epic Application Analyst. SLP ammunition: EMR superuser work, template building, documentation-workflow expertise.

**Tier 3 (real but long runway):**
- **Software Engineering** — 2 documented: self-taught SLP → $80-93k; another $96k self-taught. 1-2 years of self-teaching.
- **UX Research** — mostly aspirational; heavily oversaturated (35% more grads in 5 years vs. flat openings). One real story: rehab director → UXR via bootcamp. Portfolio of 2-4 deep case studies beats everything; healthtech UXR ($67k-154k) is the realistic niche. "One honest case study including the wrong turns is worth more than six polished AI case studies."
- **Instructional Design** — portfolio-first field: 3-5 samples in Articulate Storyline 360/Rise + process docs; spec work fully acceptable; no degree/certificate required. Vocabulary: ADDIE, Kirkpatrick levels, Mayer's principles, andragogy. 6-12 month typical timeline.
- **Pharma MSL** — effectively doctorate-gated (PhD/PharmD/MD). Don't chase it; Clinical/Rehab Liaison is the accessible cousin.

## Timelines & the honest math
- Community reality: **6-15 months** typical, some 1.5-2.5 years. Real data points: 11 months / 113 tailored apps / 7 interviews / 1 offer. 14 months of daily effort. 500+ apps for one data analyst role.
- Referrals decide most successful transitions; "generic online applications through Indeed/LinkedIn without referrals" is what fails. Expect 20-25% non-response to informational-interview asks.
- Only applications in the first 1-2 days of a posting get seen — speed matters.
- Interview prep for one successful PM transition: 15-20 hrs/round.

## Resume & hiring truths (2025-2026)
- **ATS myths:** "75% auto-rejected by ATS" traces to a 2012 sales pitch. ~92% of recruiters do NOT auto-reject on match score; ATS rank, humans reject. Modern systems match semantically. Real filters: knockout questions on application forms. Formatting matters only because broken parsing kills you: single column, standard headers, no tables/graphics.
- **AI-resume backlash:** 62% of employers reject AI-generated resumes that lack personalization; 33.5% say they spot AI resumes in <20 seconds. Tell-words: "adept," "leverage," "spearheaded," "cutting-edge," "results-driven." One hiring manager: 35% of applicants submitted identical AI-generated answers. Average 242 applications per opening (up 111% since 2022).
- **Career-changer resume:** combination format (summary + skills before history); objective statements get ~28% fewer callbacks than value-focused summaries; recruiters spend ~7.4 seconds on first pass; mirror the JD's exact vocabulary.
- **Hidden rejection reasons:** (1) retention doubt — "will they bail back to clinical?"; (2) skills described in clinical jargon the interviewer can't map; (3) no proof of investment in the new field; (4) salary mismatch fears. Never lead with burnout — frame pull, not push.

## Proven translations (from SLPs who got hired)
- Students/IEPs/treatment plans → "clinical projects" with goals, timelines, deliverables
- Caseload of X → "managed a portfolio/workbook of X concurrent clients" (keep the number!)
- Teachers/admin/parents → stakeholders; IEP meetings → cross-functional stakeholder alignment
- Therapy notes → project documentation; chart auditing → detail-oriented QA
- EMR transition help → training design + change management + "analyzed the documentation burden decrease after implementation"
- Selling treatment plans to skeptical families → persuasion, objection handling, closing
- Translating jargon for parents → plain-language communication, UX writing
- Families → customers (long-term relationship management, retention)
- "Companies want numbers — by how much did your project x improve variable y?"

## What SLPs say (community voice, paraphrase-safe)
- "Nobody really understands what SLPs do or the transferrable skills." Generic career coaches "didn't understand speech."
- Burnout compounds the job search: "burnt out brains really struggle to use executive functions, so planning and executing a career change is gonna be hard."
- Confidence collapse is common: a 5-yr SLP with $70k debt applying to $15/hr jobs.
- Guilt + community pushback for leaving is real ("toxic positivity" culture).
- "There were days when I felt like a customer service representative; a customer service representative with three degrees." (James's own line — reusable)
- One transitioner burned through a $220 resume writer, a $480 career coach, and a teacher-transition program before the thing that worked ($5,000 LinkedIn-specialist coach). The market is full of expensive, generic help.

## Interview framing that works
- Bridge structure: (1) what prompted the change framed as pull-toward, (2) evidence of preparation, (3) map 2-3 accomplishments onto the role's first-90-days problems.
- CS-specific line that lands: "I've been doing customer success my whole career — I just called it patient/family care," followed by a metric.
- Specificity beats labels: "presented outcome data to interdisciplinary teams and translated it into treatment decisions" > "communication skills."

## Additional path figures (added 2026-09-03, for the 20-path pillar update)
Sourced 2026-09-03. Use the 25th–75th percentile band where a source gives one; self-report aggregators run high.
- **Rehab Management / Leadership** — Director of Rehabilitation $98.7k–$139.4k (Glassdoor 25th–75th, avg $116.5k; Indeed avg $97.4k). Rehabilitation Program Manager $46k–$82.5k (ZipRecruiter 25th–75th; Indeed avg $81.7k). Present as a ladder: program/clinical manager first, director later. Usually starts as an internal promotion; "on-call accountability" and census pressure are the real costs. Employers: Encompass, Select Medical, Lifepoint, Sanford, Reliant.
- **Medical / Device Sales (clinical specialist track)** — Clinical Specialist, Medical Devices: base $67k–$117k + commission $0–$33k, total $57k–$125k (Payscale); ZipRecruiter avg $112.5k. Clinical specialists earn stronger base and smaller commission than pure reps because they do OR/field support and training. Speech-tech employers with this title: Lingraphica, Tobii Dynavox, PRC-Saltillo, Passy-Muir, Atos Medical. Caveats: territory travel, commission variance, a ramp year.
- **Case Manager (non-RN)** — Medical Case Manager $54.1k–$81.9k (Glassdoor 25th–75th, avg $66.3k). Top-paying sectors: government/public administration (~$82.6k median), insurance (~$80.5k). Inpatient-rehab systems advertise higher, but many postings say "RN required" — read the licence line before applying.
- **Conversation Designer** — Glassdoor "Conversational Designer" $68k–$101.8k (25th–75th, avg $82.7k); IT-sector median total pay $96.9k. The "Conversation Designer" title variant averages lower (~$63k). Small field; most postings sit inside big tech and consultancies (Amazon, Meta, Accenture).
- **Academia / Teaching** — Speech pathology faculty $37.74–$50.96/hr (ZipRecruiter 25th–75th, avg $44.25/hr ≈ $78k–$106k full-time equivalent). Adjunct is paid per course and does not add up to a salary. Full-time and tenure-track faculty want a clinical doctorate or PhD; clinical-supervisor/clinical-instructor roles at university clinics are the doorway without one. Starting faculty pay can sit below medical-SLP pay.
- **Voice & Communication Coaching (self-employed)** — no salary band: it is a business, not a job. Executive/communication coaching market rates run $200–$400/hr for mid-level coaches (simply.coach, leadersadapt 2026 guides); ZipRecruiter "executive coach" avg $122k but that pools employed and self-employed. The only honest framing is: hourly rates are high, utilisation is the whole problem, and there is no ladder. SLPs bring a real edge (voice science, accent/clarity, pragmatics) that generalist coaches lack.
- **Telehealth (bridge, not a non-clinical path)** — still clinical work; pay tracks clinical, and platform contracts often pay less than in-person. Its value is as a way to drop hours, cut commute/documentation load and free time for a transition, and as a way into non-clinical roles at the platforms themselves (Presence, eLuma, Global Teletherapy, VocoVision). Never list it as a non-clinical career.
- **Forensic SLP / Special Education Coordinator** — no reliable national band; leave out of salary tables. SpEd coordinator pay follows district administrator scales (varies by state); forensic work is hourly expert-witness billing once established.

## Networking research (added 2026-10-16, for the networking guide)
Peer-reviewed unless marked. Cite the study, not a blog that cites it.
- **Weak ties, causally:** Rajkumar et al., "A Causal Test of the Strength of Weak Ties," *Science* 377 (2022). Randomised experiments on LinkedIn's People You May Know across 20M+ members over 5 years (2B new ties, 600,000 new jobs). Moderately weak ties (some mutual connections) produced the most job mobility, more than close ties or near-strangers. Weak ties helped most in digital industries. https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/a-causal-test-of-the-strength-of-weak-ties
- **Dormant ties:** Levin, Walter & Murnighan, "Dormant Ties: The Value of Reconnecting," *Organization Science* 22(4), 2011. Executives who reconnected with contacts they hadn't spoken to in years got advice that compared favourably with current contacts': novel information plus the trust of an old relationship. https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v22y2011i4p923-939.html
- **Asking advice reads as competence:** Brooks, Gino & Schweitzer, "Smart People Ask for (My) Advice," *Management Science* 61(6), 2015. People who were asked for advice rated the asker more competent, provided the question was a hard one and went to the right person. https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v61y2015i6p1421-1435.html
- **People say yes more than you expect:** Flynn & Lake, "If You Need Help, Just Ask," *JPSP* 95(1), 2008. Askers underestimated by as much as 50% how likely others were to agree to a direct request. https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/francis-flynn-if-you-want-something-ask-it
- **Reaching out is appreciated more than you think:** Liu et al., "The Surprise of Reaching Out: Appreciated More Than We Think," *JPSP* 124(4), 2023; 13 experiments. The gap is biggest when the contact is surprising and between more distant people. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816566/
- **Compliments land better than givers predict:** Boothby & Bohns, *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 47, 2021. Givers underestimated how good the recipient would feel and overestimated how awkward it would be. https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/0698c45d-1d7b-474b-87d5-ed8c784e6172
- **Thanks doubles further help:** Grant & Gino, "A Little Thanks Goes a Long Way," *JPSP*, 2010. 66% of thanked helpers helped again vs 32% after a neutral reply (lab scenario with students). https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/frank-flynn-gratitude-gift-keeps-giving
- **Referrals:** Burks, Cowgill, Hoffman & Housman, "The Value of Hiring through Employee Referrals," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 130(2), 2015; nine large firms. Referred applicants were more likely to be hired and to accept offers at similar skill levels, and stayed longer. https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/7382 **Do not use** the circulating "4x / 70x / 60,000% more likely to be hired" referral figures: none trace to a primary source.
- **Message length (correlational, company data, emails not LinkedIn):** Boomerang, 2016. Emails of 50–125 words had the best response rates; 1–3 questions beat none or many. https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/02/7-tips-for-getting-more-responses-to-your-emails-with-data/
- **LinkedIn mechanics (third-party reports, LinkedIn changes these):** free accounts get a small number of personalised invitation notes a month, capped at 200 characters; Premium 300. Check your own account before quoting a number.
- **Thank-you timing:** university career centres (e.g. Duke Career Hub) advise a thank-you within 24 hours of an informational interview.
- **How SLPs say they got in (from content/voc-2026-09-forums.md):** an internal move to an Epic clinical analyst job inside the same hospital system; a recruiter who found a self-taught SLP on LinkedIn; a start-up marketing/product job via a community-manager role; a data analyst with a five-project portfolio; and the repeated advice that applying within 48 hours, emailing the hiring manager and referrals mattered more than any credential.
- **James's own route to Osmind (James, 2026-10-16):** congratulated an Osmind recruiter on the company's Series B with no ask; before that, joined Slack and Facebook groups for UX and content strategy and messaged SLPs who had already moved; built an Alexa skill, "Smooth Speaking", at a no-code hackathon as a proof of concept for lead generation for SLPs who treat stuttering. Built a portfolio of marketing/content projects, including copywriting and email marketing for SLP influencers and SLP businesses, before moving into marketing full time. Now a marketing manager at Osmind.

## Leaving a caseload: ethics and guilt (added 2026-10-18)
- **ASHA, Issues in Ethics: Client Abandonment** (https://www.asha.org/practice/ethics/client-abandonment/): abandonment = stopping services "without arranging for alternative care by an appropriately qualified professional" (Principle I, Rule T: reasonable notice and information about alternatives). Leaving for career moves or relocation is not unethical in itself. Clinicians are not required to stay until a replacement is found. No fixed notice period fits every case; the effort to transition clients matters more than the length. Employers should cooperate with departing clinicians. Don't remove records, protocols or materials without the administrator's consent. Vacation during the notice period can undermine the transition. Being fired is not abandonment. The page does not address employment contracts.
- **BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, SLPs** (https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/speech-language-pathologists.htm): employment projected to grow 15% from 2024 to 2034; about 13,300 openings a year on average; median pay $95,410 (May 2024). The older 19% figure is the 2022–2032 cycle; don't use it.
- **Burnout and patient safety:** Hall, Johnson, Watt, Tsipa & O'Connor, "Healthcare Staff Wellbeing, Burnout, and Patient Safety: A Systematic Review," *PLOS ONE* 11(7), 2016; 46 studies. Poor wellbeing and moderate to high burnout were associated with worse patient safety outcomes in most studies. Healthcare staff broadly, not SLP-specific; association, not causation. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/102530/
- **SLPs' own words on guilt** are in content/voc-2026-09-forums.md §2.11 (about 11 threads) and §2.14 ("you're making the shortage worse").

## Age and new-grad facts (added 2026-10-18)
- **Job changes by age (BLS, NLSY79, born 1957–1964):** 12.7 jobs from ages 18 to 56 on average; 2.9 jobs held from 35 to 44 and 2.3 from 45 to 56 (jobs spanning age groups counted once in each). https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/baby-boomers-born-from-1957-to-1964-held-an-average-of-12-7-jobs-from-ages-18-to-56.htm
- **ADEA (EEOC):** forbids age discrimination against people 40 and older, including in hiring; applies to employers with 20+ employees. https://www.eeoc.gov/age-discrimination
- **ASHA Clinical Fellowship:** at least 36 weeks and 1,260 hours, at least 5 hours a week to count, at least 80% direct clinical contact, finished within 48 months of starting; part-time allowed. https://www.asha.org/certification/clinical-fellowship/
- **CCC maintenance and reinstatement:** 30 professional development hours per three-year maintenance interval; reinstatement after a lapse needs 10 PDHs (lapsed 1 year or less), 20 (1–2 years) or 30 (2+ years), earned within 3 years of applying, plus a fee ($400 with membership at time of checking). https://www.asha.org/certification/reinstate_slp
- **PSLF (Federal Student Aid):** 120 qualifying payments, which don't have to be consecutive; time at a nonqualifying employer doesn't erase earlier qualifying payments. Qualifying employers: government organizations and 501(c)(3) nonprofits (plus certain other public-service nonprofits). https://studentaid.gov/articles/4-tips-pslf-success/
- **Mattie Murrey-Tegels** (our interview, post 19): about 25 years as a medical SLP, then professor, author, podcaster and coach in her fifties; stayed connected to the profession.
