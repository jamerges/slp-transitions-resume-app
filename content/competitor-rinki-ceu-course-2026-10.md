# Rinki Varindani Desai: "Non-Clinical Career Pathways for SLPs" (audit, 2026-10-18)

Source: SpeechPathology.com / continued.com course 11471, a one-hour live CEU webinar (James registered for the Oct 14 session). Read from the 43-slide handout, the 31-page transcript and the reference sheet in ~/Downloads. The course page itself is login-gated. Continued's terms forbid reproducing the materials, so nothing below is copied text; it's a description and a list of general lessons.

## The numbers worth knowing

- **Over 1,500 registered and over 500 attended live** (her opening, from the host). That's the clearest demand signal we have: SLPs will give an evening to this topic when it earns them a CEU.
- **Her live polls:** 81% practicing clinicians, about 6% already in a non-clinical role. On stage: about 43% "just curious", about 33% "ready to make a change", about 20% actively exploring or already moved. Her poll, her audience; use as a rough split, not a statistic.
- Format: 60 minutes, polls and chat prompts throughout, a post-test (80% to pass), "quiz hint" markers on five slides.

## How the hour runs

1. **Permission and safety.** "A safe space", identity, guilt, grief. She says plainly the talk isn't telling anyone to leave clinical care.
2. **Why now.** The traditional ladder (grad school, CF, setting, specialism, academia or leadership), then the realities: burnout, caseloads, an income ceiling (she hadn't reached six figures after 15 years), productivity pressure, low visibility for leadership roles.
3. **Her story.** A dated timeline: Mumbai at 21, graduating 2011, board certification, a dysphagia education site started in 2018 (she cites 50,000 learners and 500 universities), COVID, leaving clinical work in 2023 when pregnant, a health-tech consultancy, a doctorate. Framed with the "squiggly career" idea (Tupper and Ellis) and ikigai.
4. **What's possible.** Six transferable skills; a two-column clinical skill to business term list; a skill-to-role-family map; a grid of about 15 job titles; "lead with your clinical expertise".
5. **Nine career archetypes** (her framework, published on thenonclinicalpt.com) with a "which one are you?" prompt.
6. **Design thinking** from *Designing Your Life* (Burnett and Evans): empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test. She maps it onto the plan-of-care loop clinicians already run.
7. **The 7R framework** (her framework, published on ASHA's career portal): reflect, reframe, research, reach out, reimagine, risk-take, renew. She names reflect and reach out as the two that matter most, and warns against "spray and pray" applying.
8. **Four barriers:** "I'm just a clinician", "I don't have a ___ background", "am I abandoning my patients?", "I don't know where to begin".
9. **Close:** three takeaways (your career can evolve, you're not starting over, test before you commit) and three next steps (research one path, connect with one person, run one 30-day experiment).
10. **Q&A:** can you blend archetypes; is this realistic for new grads (yes, at entry level: customer success, sales, marketing, associate PM, ops, research assistant); am I too late (no).

## Style

- **Warm and permission-giving.** About a third of the hour is identity and feelings before any job is named. "Expansion, not escape" is the spine.
- **Framework-heavy.** Ikigai, squiggly careers, design thinking, nine archetypes, seven Rs. Several are borrowed and credited; two are her own named IP.
- **Participatory.** A poll at the start, a fill-in-the-blank ("in my next chapter I want more ___"), "drop a word in the chat", thumbs-up check-ins, reading the chat back aloud.
- **Biography as credibility.** Credentials first (SLPD, CCC-SLP, BCS-S, Mass General Brigham), then a long personal story.
- **Light on hard numbers.** A "9 to 12 months" job-search figure with no source, "90,000 hours at work", her own business metrics. No salaries, no named employers, no message or résumé examples.
- **Visuals:** AI-assisted images of herself on several slides, stock imagery, alliteration (the seven Rs).
- **Platform-bound.** A CEU course can't sell, so it ends on LinkedIn and her website, not an offer.

## General lessons to adapt and remix

Ideas, formats and public frameworks only. **Never use** her nine archetypes, the 7R names or structure, her story, her slide wording, or her unsourced 9-to-12-month figure (ours is 6–15 months, sourced).

1. **The CEU channel is the big one.** 1,500 registrants for a free-to-members CEU hour beats anything we've run. Options:
   - **Present on SpeechPathology.com or another CEU platform.** They pay an honorarium and handle the ASHA paperwork. Our angle is different enough: what the move pays and how long it takes, with documented SLP outcomes. Pitch a course, not a sales talk.
   - **Make the Rinki workshop a CEU.** If a joint session goes ahead, run it through a platform that grants CEUs. SLPs already budget for CEUs, which takes care of the "one more person selling to SLPs" objection.
   - **Become an ASHA CE provider later.** Only worth it once a course exists.
2. **Open with permission before any data.** She spends 15 minutes on identity before a job title. Our five-stages article does this for stages 1–3. Lead the Getting Started webinar script (content/course/getting-started-webinar-script.md) the same way: name the guilt, then the numbers.
3. **Build participation into everything live.** A first poll ("where are you right now?" mapped to our five stages), one fill-in-the-blank, one "type one word". Use the same prompts in Instagram story polls and as reply-to-this questions in emails. Every answer is voice-of-customer data.
4. **Tell your path as a dated timeline.** Hers works because it has years and turns in it. Yours now has real beats: SLP, the UX and content-strategy groups, messaging former SLPs, the Alexa skill at a no-code hackathon, freelance copy and email for SLP businesses, the Series B note, marketing manager at Osmind. Ask James for the years before drawing it.
5. **Map skills to role families in one view.** She puts skill to business term to job family side by side. Our transferable-skills post has the translations; add the third column (which of our 20 paths uses it) and link each to the path page.
6. **"Test before you commit" as a named step.** Design thinking's prototype idea is public, from Burnett and Evans. Ours should be concrete: a one-week build (course lesson 5.2), one 15-minute conversation, one volunteer project, with James's hackathon as the worked example. Call it a 30-day test in our own words; don't borrow her "prototype your pivot" line.
7. **End every talk and article on three tiny next steps.** Hers: research one, connect with one, run one experiment. Ours: take the quiz to get one path, send one message from the template, start one small build. The pattern is generic; the steps are ours.
8. **Turn her Q&A into articles we don't have yet.** All three are already in our forum research:
   - "Am I too old to leave SLP?" (the 30 and 40–50 "age walls")
   - "Leaving SLP as a new grad or during your CF" (entry-level roles; finish the CF for the CCC)
   - "Part-time clinical plus something else" (portfolio and hybrid careers, still an open gap)
9. **Answer the "am I abandoning my patients?" fear head-on.** It's on her barrier slide and in our forum research. Our stage map names the guilt as "leaving wastes the degree", and lesson 1.3 does the sunk-cost math. Nothing in the course or on the site names the other half: leaving the kids on your caseload, the patients who asked for you. That deserves its own short piece, in the five-stages voice, and a line in lesson 1.3 or 1.6.
10. **Use AI as a rehearsal partner, with limits.** She suggests asking a chatbot what a product manager's day looks like. Our lesson 3.8 goes further (what to hand the model and what to keep). Worth a short public article.
11. **Credentials versus a lived switch.** She leads with SLPD, CCC-SLP, BCS-S and Mass General Brigham; we lead with having made the move. Keep the author line ("former SLP, now a marketing manager at Osmind") and use real photos only: no AI-generated images of James.

## Her angles and frameworks to revisit (2026-10-18, James asked for the full list)

Two of these are her own named IP (the nine archetypes and the 7R roadmap). Revisit them to understand what they do for a reader, then build our own equivalent in our own words; never reuse the names, the categories or the order. The borrowed frameworks are public and fine to use with credit to the original authors, not to her.

### Her own frameworks

1. **Nine SLP career archetypes:** clinical specialist, researcher, educator, leader, entrepreneur, innovator, advocate, connector, multi-hyphenate. A "which one are you?" identity sort by what energizes you, before any job title. **Why it works:** it gives a stage-1 to 3 reader a flattering, low-stakes first answer. **Our version:** we already sort by energy and dials (lessons 1.4 and 1.5) and the quiz names a path. What we lack is the short, shareable identity result; a one-line "you're drawn to ___ work" sentence on the quiz result could do that job.
2. **The 7R roadmap:** reflect, reframe, research, reach out, reimagine, risk-take, renew. A process map with a mnemonic. **Why it works:** it's memorable and it says "this is a sequence, not a leap". **Notice:** our course's eight modules (Ground, Explore, Connect, Translate, Test, Leap, After) already cover the same arc. Ours has no mnemonic. Don't make one up to compete; lead with the stops and what each one produces.

### Borrowed frameworks she uses (public; credit the originals)

3. **Ikigai:** what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, what you can be paid for. Popular, and widely misattributed; if used, present it as a reflection prompt, not science.
4. **The squiggly career** (Helen Tupper and Sarah Ellis, *The Squiggly Career*, 2020): careers move sideways and back, and the through-line shows up looking back. **Use:** permission for non-linear paths, and "what's your through-line?" as a prompt for the About section and the bridge statement.
5. **Designing Your Life** (Bill Burnett and Dave Evans, 2016): empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test. The book also has "prototype conversations" and "odyssey plans" (three alternative five-year lives), which she didn't cover and which suit our Explore module.
6. **Portfolio careers:** several part-time roles at once, framed as a deliberate design rather than a failure to commit. Connects to our unwritten part-time and fractional article.

### Angles worth revisiting

7. **"Expanding, not escaping."** Her spine: you don't have to reject the clinical identity to add another. She lists six forms (deepen, expand, merge, pivot, reinvent, grow). **For us:** it's the gentlest possible frame for stage-1 to 3 readers and the "you're making the shortage worse" crowd. Our brand is more direct ("you're allowed to want out"); use her angle for the hybrid and part-time pieces, not the main voice.
8. **The permission slip.** An opening slide that says "you're in the right place if…" with five reasons (curious, want flexibility, more impact, more income, a new season of life). A good format for the top of the five-stages article or the webinar.
9. **Career design as a plan of care.** Hypothesis, goals, collect data, adjust weekly. The most SLP-native metaphor in the talk, and it's an observation anyone could make. We can use it in our own words: "you'd never write a five-year plan of care on day one."
10. **Lead with your clinical expertise.** You won't out-PM a 20-year product manager, but they can't learn what a caseload is. Positions the license as the edge rather than the baggage. Fits our "keep your title" résumé rule.
11. **"Spray and pray."** Her name for mass-applying without direction. Our 113-applications and 500-applications numbers prove the cost; the phrase is common enough to use.
12. **Seasons of life.** Motherhood, caregiving, a partner's job loss, as legitimate reasons on their own. Matches our "bad season" verdict lesson; her version is warmer and validates without diagnosing.
13. **The through-line.** Looking back, connect the moves with one sentence about what stayed constant. A strong prompt for the LinkedIn About section and the "why are you leaving?" answer.
14. **Different rooms, different identities.** She's a clinician at ASHA, a founder at a startup meetup, a consultant in health-tech rooms (her neurosurgeon-who-DJs anecdote). An angle for the identity lesson (1.6): you can introduce yourself differently in each room without lying.
15. **Four barriers:** "I'm just a clinician", "I don't have a ___ background", "am I abandoning my patients?", "I don't know where to begin". All four are in our forum research too. We now answer the third (lesson 1.6, the abandonment article). The second deserves a short piece: what actually gets taught on the job.
16. **AI as a career brainstorming partner.** Feed it your non-negotiables and ask for ten titles to explore. Our lesson 3.8 already covers the safer version (what to hand the model and what to keep).
17. **The visibility gap.** SLPs get passed over for rehab director, innovation and C-suite roles despite being qualified. An angle for the leadership and rehab-management path page.
18. **Advancing the profession.** Framing a move out as putting SLPs in rooms where they're missing (health tech, policy, product). Answers the guilt with a mission. Use sparingly; it can sound grand.
19. **New grads and late movers.** New grads should aim at entry roles (customer success, sales, marketing, associate PM, ops, research assistant); nobody is too old. Both are unwritten articles for us.
20. **Volunteering as a test.** State associations and ASHA committees as low-risk experiments that also build a network. A good addition to our build-something section.
21. **Independent consulting.** Health-tech consulting as a path, and a community of women who left corporate careers to consult on their own hours. We don't have consulting or fractional work as a path; worth a look once we have a documented SLP example.
22. **Where she publishes.** ASHA's career portal (the 7R piece), the ASHA Leader, thenonclinicalpt.com (the archetypes), and CEU platforms. Those are distribution channels, and ASHA's own outlets give her credibility we don't have yet. A pitch to the ASHA Leader or the career portal is worth trying.
