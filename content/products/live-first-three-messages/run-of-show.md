## 0:00–0:05 · Welcome and where everyone is

- Hi, I'm James. Former SLP, now a marketing manager at Osmind. Camera's optional, chat is the main channel.
- **Poll:** "Where are you right now?" Four options from our stages: haven't told anyone / guilty about it / want permission / ready but don't know where to start (and "already applying" as a fifth if Zoom allows).
- **Chat prompt:** "Type one job title you're curious about. Two is fine." Read a few aloud.
- The promise for the hour: by 9:00 you'll have sent three messages, and you'll have a list for the next two weeks.

## 0:05–0:15 · Why people, and what got me hired

- Most SLPs who make this move get there through a person. Two numbers: one SLP sent 113 tailored applications over eleven months for a single offer; another sent more than 500 in six months before landing a remote analyst job. Both got there. The volume was the price of having nobody inside.
- Referrals change which pile your résumé lands in (economists studying nine large companies found referred applicants more likely to be hired and to accept).
- **My story:** the Slack and Facebook groups for UX and content strategy; messaging SLPs who'd already moved; the no-code hackathon Alexa skill; and the congratulations note to an Osmind recruiter after the Series B, with no ask in it.
- Research line for the no-ask note: people appreciate being contacted more than the sender expects, especially out of the blue.

## 0:15–0:25 · Build your list of ten (exercise)

- Slide: the five places to look. Your grad cohort. The reps who walk into your building (AAC, EMR trainer, the ed-tech rep). The ones who already left (the CF you supervised). The non-clinical teams at your own employer. Everyone outside speech.
- Research line: people you half-know produce more job moves than close friends or strangers (the LinkedIn study of 20 million members).
- **Exercise, 5 minutes:** write ten names or roles. Don't judge them yet. Chat prompt at the end: "How many did you get?"

## 0:25–0:35 · Find them on LinkedIn

- The two searches, on a slide (no live profiles on the recording):
  1. Two phrases in quotes: `"speech-language pathologist" "customer success"`, swapping in your title.
  2. A company's page, then **People**, then scan for CCC-SLP or a speech background.
- One tip: free accounts get only a handful of personalized connection notes a month. Save them for the people you most want; connect without a note for everyone else, and send the real message after they accept.
- **Exercise, 4 minutes:** run one search and add three names to your list.

## 0:35–0:45 · The message

- Slide: the message that gets ignored next to the one that gets answered (from the networking guide).
- The five things: names the move they made; asks for a small, fixed amount of time; asks for no job; easy to decline; easy to say yes to.
- Show the template in the doc. Show the no-ask version too: congratulating someone on real news (a launch, a promotion, a funding round) and leaving it there.
- Keep it to about 50–125 words with one question at the end.

## 0:45–1:00 · Write and send three (the core)

- **Timer: 15 minutes, music on, cameras optional.** "Write three. Send them. Type SENT in the chat after each one."
- At the 5-minute mark: invite one volunteer to paste a draft (names removed). Edit it live against the five things. Do this two or three times, briefly.
- At 13 minutes: "Two minutes. If one's 80% there, send it. A sent message beats a perfect draft."
- Read out the SENT count in the chat. This is the moment people will remember.

## 1:00–1:08 · What happens next

- Silence is normal: roughly one in four or five won't reply. Send one follow-up about a week later, then let it rest.
- Research line: people say yes to direct requests far more often than askers predict (by as much as half).
- When someone says yes, the two questions for the last two minutes of the call: "Is there anyone else you'd talk to if you were me?" and "If a [role] opens on your team, would you be open to me sending you the posting?"
- Thank-you note within a day that names one thing they said. Thanked helpers were about twice as likely to help again in one study.
- The people tracker: who you messaged, when, and who's due a follow-up.

## 1:08–1:15 · Questions and close

- Open questions from the chat. Have answers ready for: "What if my whole network is SLPs?" (the list exercise), "Is it weird to message strangers?" (no, and here's the research), "What if they ask for my résumé?" (send it, and thank them).
- **Close with three small steps for the next two weeks:** send the other seven messages on your list, follow up once on anyone quiet after a week, book one conversation.
- Replay and templates arrive tomorrow. One-question survey link in the chat: "What would you want from a second session?"
