# The Transition OS tracker

The people list inside the course (Module 3) is the working copy: it counts your week, tells you who is due, and fills the message templates with each person's name. **Download as a sheet (CSV)** under that list gives you this same People sheet with your rows in it, for Sheets or Notion. The three CSVs here are the blank versions.

Three sheets. Import each CSV as its own tab, or paste them into Notion as
databases. Everything in row 2 of each file is an example: delete it once you
have your own.

## Google Sheets
1. New spreadsheet, then File, Import, Upload, and pick `people`.
2. Choose "Insert new sheet" so it lands on its own tab. Rename the tab People.
3. Repeat for `applications` and `weekly`.
4. Select row 1 and choose View, Freeze, 1 row.

## Notion
1. Add a new page, type `/table` and choose Table view, then Import, CSV.
2. One import per file gives you three linked databases.
3. On the People database, set `Status` to a Select property with the options
   Cold, Messaged, Warm, Follow up once, Closed.
4. On the Applications database, set `Status` to Select with Applied, Screen,
   Interview, Offer, No.

## How to actually use it

**People** is the one that matters most. Referrals decide more of these moves
than applications do. Fill in `Who they suggested next` after
every call, because that column is where a network comes from.

**Applications** exists to give you a ratio rather than a feeling. Once you
have twenty rows you can see which stage is actually broken: applications
without screens means the résumé still reads clinical, screens without
interviews means the bridge statement is doing the wrong work, and interviews
without offers usually means the proof problem. `Days between` is worth
tracking on its own, because a tailored application sent on day one beats a
perfect one sent on day nine.

**Weekly** takes two minutes on a Friday and is the only honest way to tell a
slow month from a stalled search. One documented transition ran to 113 tailored
applications, 7 interviews and 1 offer over eleven months. Seeing the numbers
climb is easier than remembering how the week felt.
